## Setting

Work at one site with `H = C^2`, `P(n) = (I + n dot sigma)/2`, and supplied effect domain `S = {cP(n): 0<c<=1} union {cI: 0<c<=1}`. An **indexed menu** is a finite tuple `(E_1,...,E_k)` summing to `I`, with repeated entries allowed. A menu-independent **indexed-effect grade** `w:S->[0,1]` gives `q_j=w(E_j)` and obeys `sum_j q_j=1`. Write `W(c,n)=c/2+K(cn)` and `u(c)=c+utilde(c)`.

The unindexed Record-content distribution is a different object: `p(E)=sum_{j:E_j=E}q_j`. Its support consists of contents with positive summed mass. Equal matrices do not encode the index `j`; implementing or registering that extra label is an open physical bridge. If a supplied state gives some `q_j=0`, that index is not in probability support even though its effect remains in the algebraic tuple. A strictly positive-support reading would need its own positivity restriction.

For example, the actual laws return `(I/2,I/2)`, `(P(m)/2,P(m)/2,P(-m))`, and `(P(m)/4,P(m)/2,P(m)/4,P(-m))`. At `rho=I/2` their indexed masses sum to one; assigning one grade to each distinct matrix gives only `1/2`, `3/4`, and `7/8`. Summing duplicates restores unit mass but generally makes a content's probability depend on its menu multiplicity. Consequently a menu-independent grade on effects is **not automatically** a menu-independent probability on Record contents.

The tuple constructions depend on six supplied neighboring records. They do not prove one-site registration or a sequential formation process. Exact rational arithmetic handles the finite algebra; the separately labelled float64 block checks one declared model.

## Appendix A — Self-contained Conditional Continuum Proof

All statements concern an indexed-effect grade `w:S->[0,1]`, normalized on **every real-parameter tuple** in the specified family. Finite rational grids provide checks of examples, not these universal hypotheses. `F_CONT` includes all binaries, binary scalar coins and all non-collinear balanced ternaries. `F_A` adds all collinear triples and mixed scalar triples; `F_4` also adds all positive four-outcome collinear splits. The singleton `I` has grade one.

**A1. Real-ray homogeneity for `F_4`.** Fix a unit `n`. For `a,b>0` with `a+b<1`, subtract normalization of `((a+b)P(n),(1-a-b)P(n),P(-n))` from that of `(aP(n),bP(n),(1-a-b)P(n),P(-n))`. This gives `W(a,n)+W(b,n)=W(a+b,n)`. At `a+b=1`, subtract the binary from the collinear triple. Thus `W` is additive on the interval, nonnegative and bounded. Additivity gives `W(j/k,n)=(j/k)W(1,n)`; nonnegativity makes `W` monotone, and squeezing any real `c` between rational sequences gives `W(c,n)=cW(1,n)`. The mixed tuple then gives `u(1-c)=1-c`. No continuity or measurability assumption is used.

**A2. Homogeneous great-circle result and gluing.** Assume rank-one homogeneity `W(c,n)=cW(1,n)`, supplied for `F_CONT` or proved by A1 for `F_4`. Write `f(n)=W(1,n)-1/2`; the binary gives `f(-n)=-f(n)`. On a fixed plane define `G(0)=0` and `G(x)=|x|f(x/|x|)` for nonzero `x`. It is homogeneous for positive real scales by definition and odd. For nonparallel `x,y`, scale the three vectors `x,y,-x-y` by `2/(|x|+|y|+|x+y|)`. Each resulting length is strictly below one, their sum is two, and their vector sum is zero, so they define one allowed non-collinear ternary. Its normalization, after subtracting the trace contribution, says `G(x)+G(y)=G(x+y)`. For parallel vectors the same identity follows directly from signed real homogeneity. For an orthonormal basis of the plane, additivity gives `G(x_1 e_1+x_2 e_2)=x_1G(e_1)+x_2G(e_2)`. Hence `f` is a linear form on the entire great circle, with no regularity assumption.

To glue, let `a_i=f(e_i)` in a fixed spatial orthonormal basis. For `n=(x,y,z)` with `r=sqrt(x^2+y^2)>0`, circle linearity on the equator gives `f((x,y,0)/r)=(a_1x+a_2y)/r`; the circle through that direction and `e_3` then gives `f(n)=a_1x+a_2y+a_3z`. The poles follow from oddness. The range at `n=±a/|a|` gives `|a|<=1/2`, and `rho=(I+2a dot sigma)/2` is the unique density matrix with `W(c,n)=tr(rho cP(n))`. For `F_CONT` alone this argument determines the rank-one grades under their supplied homogeneity; its independent scalar-coin grades require homogeneity also on scalar effects to infer `u(c)=c`.

**A3. Regularity stated before the `F_A` mode argument.** For a global pointwise theorem here assume `n->W(c,n)` is continuous on `S^2` for each fixed `0<c<=1`. This is an additional mathematical hypothesis, not a framework consequence. The intermediate Fourier calculation needs only integrability of each fixed-scale restriction to every great circle. Under that weaker hypothesis the conclusion below is only a.e. on each circle at each scale; no global sphere-a.e. or pointwise gluing claim is made from those a.e. restrictions alone. The historical weaker sphere-measurable claim remains unestablished in this corrected packet.

Fix an oriented circle, with angular coordinate `phi`, and put `h_k(c)=(1/(2pi)) integral K(c n(phi)) exp(-ik phi) dphi`. Grades in `[0,1]` bound these coefficients. Rotating every normalized tuple and integrating gives the corresponding mode identity. In particular the binary gives `h_0(1)=0`, and the collinear tuple gives `h_0(c)+h_0(1-c)=0`.

**A4. Eliminate the scalar mode before asserting oddness.** For any positive `x,y,z` with `x+y+z=1`, lengths `(1-x,1-y,1-z)` lie strictly below one and form a nondegenerate perimeter-two triangle. Its zero-mode identity and the collinear relation imply `h_0(x)+h_0(y)+h_0(z)=0`. Subtract `h_0(x+y)+h_0(z)=0` to obtain interval additivity. A bounded additive function on this interval is linear: if `|h_0|<=M`, then for `0<t<1/N`, `|h_0(t)|=|h_0(Nt)|/N<=M/N`, so it is continuous at zero and rational approximation determines it. Since `h_0(1)=0`, it vanishes. Averaging the mixed tuple now gives `c+u(1-c)=1`, hence `u(c)=c`. Only now the **pointwise** mixed identity gives `K(cn)+K(-cn)=0`. Thus all even angular modes, including zero, vanish.

**A5. All odd modes, without a finite mode cutoff.** Let `A_i` be the interior angles opposite triangle side lengths `c_i`, with `c_1+c_2+c_3=2`. A triangle and its reflected copy give two equations

```text
sum_i h_k(c_i) exp(+ik alpha_i) = 0,
sum_i h_k(c_i) exp(-ik alpha_i) = 0.
```

For odd `k`, the null vector of these two phase rows is proportional to `(sin(kA_1),sin(kA_2),sin(kA_3))` when the rows have rank two. At `k=1` this rank condition holds on every nondegenerate triangle; the sine law gives `h_1(c_i)/c_i` equal for its three sides. Any two lengths in `(0,1)` connect through a third length sufficiently close to one, so `h_1(c)=lambda_C c` throughout that interval. The collinear identity at `c=1/2` and oddness give `h_1(1)=2h_1(1/2)=lambda_C`.

For each odd `k>=3`, take `A_1=pi/k`. As `A_2` varies, the first side ranges over `(m_k,1)`, where `m_k=2 sin(pi/(2k))/(1+sin(pi/(2k)))`. The two rows then force `h_k(c_1)=0` except at the finitely many shapes at which both remaining sine entries also vanish. At `k=3` no such shape lies in the open interval: its sole interior equal-angle shape is the endpoint `c_1=2/3`. Thus `h_3` is zero on `(2/3,1)`; any `a<2/3` can be completed with two sides in that interval, forcing `h_3(a)=0`. The endpoint `a=2/3` can then be completed by one side above and one below `2/3`, both already zero.

For odd `k>=5`, `m_k<1/2`. For **any** target `a` in `(0,1)`, choose `b,d` in `(m_k,1)` with `b+d=2-a`, avoiding the finitely many exceptional lengths for both. Such choices form a nonempty open interval since `2-a>1>2m_k` and `2-a<2`. The triangle identity has only the `h_k(a)` term left and forces it to zero. The collinear identity ties the endpoint `a=1` as before. Negative modes are complex conjugates because `K` is real. Thus every mode other than `±1` vanishes at every scale; this is an all-k argument, not an extrapolation from `k<=12`.

**A6. Fourier conclusion and its precise boundary.** Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients for an integrable circle function gives `K(c n)=c beta_C dot n` a.e. on that circle at each scale. One elementary justification is convolution with the Fejer kernels: their trigonometric-polynomial convolutions converge in `L1`, and here contain only the two first modes. Under the stated continuity hypothesis the equality holds at **every** direction of that circle. The same `beta_C` works for all scales by A5. All circle restrictions of `f(n)=K(n)` are then pointwise linear, so A2's coordinate gluing yields a single `beta` and the range gives `|beta|<=1/2`. This proves the conditional global `F_A` theorem. No continuity in the scale was assumed. The four-outcome result uses A1/A2 and needs neither Fourier analysis nor angular regularity.
