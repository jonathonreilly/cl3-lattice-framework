## The stipulated levels and tick models, declared in full

All levels and kernels are supplied finite models, with `g>=0`, real finite h and `0<=q<=1`. A different physical proposal does not inherit these arbitrary choices or every comparison target.

1. **GLUED.** The condition is a **support condition**: every configuration violating it carries odds **exactly 0** and is never registered. Two forms, both from PR #7891: **`C_comp`**, every record has at least one adjacent
   record; **`C_rig`**, every pair adjacent now stays adjacent. The emergent instance is Gauss's law `G_v = (div E)_v - rho_v = 0` on the cube (`T4`).
2. **BREAKABLE.** The condition is an **energy cost**, `E(S) = -g A(S)` with `A(S)` the number of adjacent record pairs. With `B(S) = (n-1) - A(S)` the number of adjacencies the group has lost relative to its maximally
   bonded form -- exact for `n <= 3` on this bipartite lattice, where `A <= n-1` -- the odds of `S` are suppressed by `e^{-g}` **per broken adjacency**: `pi(S) ~ e^{-g B(S)}`. Low suppression under most conditions, raised
   under some, is exactly the dial `g`.
3. **FREE.** No energetic preference: `g=0`, but the set representation still excludes coincident records. The BR proposal retains null ticks and hard-core exclusion.
4. **`K1` the single-record tick.** Exactly one record shifts to a nearest-neighbour site, uniformly over the admissible shifts. At **reach `r`** the target is the record's site plus `r*d`. `D_1 = 1/6` for one record.
5. **`KB(r)` the neighbourhood tick.** The whole support set shifts by `r` units **as a block**, uniformly over the six unit directions.
6. **`KM(q)` the mixed tick.** With odds `q` a `KB(1)` block shift, with odds `1-q` a `K1` single shift.
7. **`BR(g, h, reach)` the breakable tick.** A record is chosen with odds `1/n`; a direction with odds `e^{h}/(5 + e^{h})` for `+x` and `1/(5 + e^{h})` for each of the other five; the target is the record's site plus
   `reach*d`. A target carrying a record makes the tick **null**. Otherwise the shift is registered with odds `min(1, e^{-g dB})`. At `h = 0` the proposal is symmetric, so `pi(S) ~ e^{-g B(S)}` is exactly stationary.

**Kernel and time convention.** K1 is uniform over admissible moves, with a null tick when none exists. BR instead attempts a particle/direction proposal and includes occupied-target and Metropolis nulls. These are different stochastic clocks even on the same move graph. The unexcluded independent reference with exactly one particle moving per tick has `D_free(n)=1/(6n^2)`, hence `D_free(2)=D1/4`. If both independent particles move each tick the reference is `D1/2`. Actual BR at g=0 retains hard-core exclusion and has `510/511` of the former reference.

The separately declared extension `KMBR(q,g,h)=q KB_h+(1-q)BR(g,h,1)` uses the same tilted six direction probabilities in both components. It differs from the glued `KM(q)=qKB+(1-q)K1`. ## Corollary -- what this says about the owner's two points

1. A supplied support condition and a finite Metropolis cost are different models. Support alone does not choose a transition law; no value of g, formation probability or clock follows from the axioms.
2. For the specified dimer/bent trimer, K1 cages and KB translates; reach-two dimer leapfrogs are a further positive alternative. BR transport requires rupture for these intact shapes. These examples do not classify all group motion or all possible ticks.
3. Uniform tilt leaves positive rupture. The h* formula compares means; ring rows compare first-hit events. Neither proves certain survival, permanent merging, or a differential-force mechanism.
4. The cube relation constrains individual flips and oriented hopping pairs, but also permits link-only face cycles. Its support does not supply amplitudes, weights or an implementation.
5. Static admissibility may be considered without dynamics, but diffusion, lifetime and hitting probabilities then have no meaning until a new transition/formation law is supplied. Permanent physical Records require a separate bridge, not a change of wording.

## Reading, not theorem -- the whole thing in plain words

A condition can exclude a configuration, while a finite cost can only suppress it in the specified Metropolis model. What a group does also depends on how moves are proposed and counted. The finite examples separate those choices and retain the useful cage and transport calculations. They do not show that permanent fine-site Records move, merge or implement these supplied stochastic laws.
