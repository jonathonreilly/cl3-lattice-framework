## Exact fresh-support obstruction for the historical coefficient algorithm

Let a selected half seed introduce a raw coordinate absent from the union of every earlier beta and Gamma-beta support. Its beta coefficient on that coordinate remains exactly plus or minus one half, because every subtraction term is supported on the old union. A fresh insertion similarly retains coefficient one. A new half label that reuses old raw support does not satisfy this hypothesis.

For a saved positive pivot interval \([L,U]\), a fresh half coefficient has magnitude \(1/(2\sqrt r)\). The image interval width is
\[
w=\frac1{2\sqrt L}-\frac1{2\sqrt U}
 =\frac{U-L}{2\sqrt L\sqrt U(\sqrt U+\sqrt L)}
 \ge\frac{U-L}{4U\sqrt U}.
\]
Replacing \(\sqrt U\) by any certified upper bound B preserves the lower bound \((U-L)/(4UB)\). An insertion doubles it. For the historical192-bit interval arithmetic with \(S=2^{192}\) and integer pivot endpoints l,u, the square-root endpoints are
\[
a=\lfloor\sqrt{lS}\rfloor,\qquad b=\lceil\sqrt{uS}\rceil.
\]
The forced normalized half-coefficient interval has integer endpoints
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{S^2}{2b}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\left\lceil\frac{S^2}{2a}\right\rceil.
\]
Negative half coefficients have the same width. If a is zero, the positive-divisor guard fails instead. These are one-coordinate consequences of the actual recurrence and outward primitives; no full coefficient reconstruction is needed.
