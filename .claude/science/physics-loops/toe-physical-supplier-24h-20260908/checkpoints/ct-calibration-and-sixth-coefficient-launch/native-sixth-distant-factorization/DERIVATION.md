# Distant sixth-order cancellation by an even conserved Clifford symmetry

Claim, conditional on the supplied isolated uniform L4 endpoint and its established dictionary: the sixth-order spectator bilinear for vertex separation greater than three vanishes. This is a finite coefficient statement. It does not evaluate the remaining adjacent/distance-three coefficients. A proof by generic linked-cluster factorization is unnecessary.

Let beta denote spectator Majoranas, gamma active Majoranas, and J the initial active complex structure. For vertex v let S_v contain v and its six neighbors and W_v=span(S_v,J S_v). On this endpoint J²=-I and W_v has dimension12. For distance(v,w)>3, W_v and W_w are orthogonal J-invariant spaces. The initial quadratic Hamiltonian splits on these spaces and their orthogonal complement. Any sign changes on edges incident to v change only the quadratic form on W_v. The corresponding assertion holds at w. All active quadratic forms, including the unchanged complement, are even operators.

Why these restricted perturbations suffice: a nonadjacent pair cut has twelve distinct edges. A sixth-order word returning this cut must use each exactly once. Each electric insertion pairs incident edges. At separation>3 no edge at v shares an endpoint with an edge at w. Thus all six insertions lie separately in the two stars. Introduce independent coefficients for all within-star electric pairs and set every other perturbation to zero. The desired multidegree coefficient in the full Hamiltonian is unchanged. Scalar pieces of D cannot participate in a twelve-edge sixth-order word. Canonical folded terms are included by considering the exact canonical effective operator of this restricted finite family.

Use the unreduced sign-toggle cover: each star has its even subsets of six edges as link labels, and an electric pair shifts the label by its two edges. The active Hamiltonian at each label is the quadratic form with those bond signs. Gauge-equivalent labels differing by the complete star are identified by the involution

F_v = (complement-toggle at v) (-i gamma_v beta_v),

up to a fixed harmless convention sign; similarly F_w. These are even commuting involutions, commute with the covered Hamiltonian, and implement the physical singleton Gauss closure. Other vertices can be gauge-fixed once. The remaining graph after deleting v,w is connected on L4 (independently checked for every separation>3 pair); consequently the only cuts supported in this union of stars are generated by these two singleton cuts. Thus this cover with the two identifications reproduces the physical local flux orbit rather than treating gauge-equivalent sectors as independent. Retain the global even fermion parity throughout.

Let C_v be the Hermitian active chirality on the twelve-dimensional W_v, with C_v²=1. It anticommutes with each gamma in W_v and commutes with all even active operators supported there. Since W_v is J-invariant, its unique active vacuum is an eigenvector of C_v, with eigenvalue c_v=±1. Choose any spectator beta_z distinct from beta_v,beta_w. Such spectators exist in the N=64 carrier. The even Hermitian involution

T_v = i C_v beta_v beta_z

commutes with the exact covered Hamiltonian: its active chirality commutes with every quadratic term, its spectator factors are absent from that Hamiltonian, and electric label shifts carry no active/spectator operators on the cover. It also commutes with F_v: C_v anticommutes with gamma_v while beta_v beta_z anticommutes with beta_v, giving two minus signs. It commutes with F_w and global parity. Therefore it descends to the physical fixed-parity Hilbert space. This step is the essential gauge/parity check; an unphysical lone spectator Majorana would not suffice.

The initial isolated projection P commutes with T_v. The full spectral projector P(g), and the canonical polar isometry P(g)P[P P(g)P]^{-1/2}, commute/intertwine T_v because they are spectral or positive functional calculus of operators commuting with it. Thus the COMPLETE canonical effective Hamiltonian commutes with its restriction to P. In the q=0 representative of the initial flux sector, that restriction is i c_v beta_v beta_z. The gauge-related representatives obey the same identity by the preceding intertwining, so no representative-dependent sign is discarded.

By the independently proved cut-through-twelve classification, the target sixth-order term for cut{v,w} is a coefficient times beta_v beta_w, with no other spectator monomial sharing its cut. Conjugation by i beta_v beta_z changes its sign. Hence commutation forces its coefficient to zero. This argument covers folded terms and canonical normalization automatically; it is stronger than asserting individual resolvent words factor. It does not require a tensor-product decomposition of the globally parity-restricted physical Hilbert space.

Together with the separately reviewed same-sublattice reality argument, only distance-one and distance-three bilinears remain possible on uniform L4. This is a support reduction, not a nonzero coefficient, gap, phase or thermodynamic claim. No assumption of vanishing distance-three coupling is made: its W spaces need not be orthogonal, and T_v then fails to commute with the other defect Hamiltonian.

Independent controls check complement connectivity for all distant L4 pairs and the Clifford commutation signs for every active bilinear and possible local closure generator. These controls do not solve a resolvent or replace the analytic spectral-isometry argument. Root's prior disjoint-factor suggestion was disclosed before this derivation; the conserved-symmetry construction is this attempt's route.
