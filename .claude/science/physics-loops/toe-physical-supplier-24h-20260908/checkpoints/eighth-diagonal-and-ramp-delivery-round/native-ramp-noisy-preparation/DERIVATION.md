# Local stochastic preparation errors in the supplied ramp protocol

## Domain and result

Keep the reviewed ramp Hamiltonian, profile, finite even cubic torus and final dressed observable unchanged. Write dimensionless time u=Ut and total time

\[
T_u=\epsilon^{-1}+\tau\epsilon^{-4},\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq\tau_*.
\]

Let rho0 be any ice-supported density matrix, with arbitrary correlations. At preparation only, apply the tensor product of channels

\[
\Phi=\prod_e\Phi_e,\qquad \Phi_e=(1-p_e)\mathrm{id}+p_e\mathcal N_e,
\quad 0\leq p_e\leq p,
\]

where each N_e is an arbitrary CPTP map on the one-edge qubit, tensored with the identity elsewhere. This is a product channel; it is not a model with correlated fault choices, shared ancillary memory or errors during evolution. It permits coherent, nonunital and charge-creating errors at a faulty edge. No independence of rho0 is needed.

For a fixed local O_X, measured as Y14† O_X Y14 after the actual protocol, there are volume-independent constants such that

\[
|\langle O_{\mathrm{lab}}\rangle_{\Phi(\rho_0)}-
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0 e^{i\tau J_4}O_Xe^{-i\tau J_4})|
\leq \|O_X\|\left[C_{X,\tau_*}\epsilon^2+
C_X p\{1+C\epsilon T_u\}^3\right].
\]

The noise term can also be capped by 2||O_X||. In particular, at bounded natural ring time it is O(p epsilon^-9), with no necessary logarithmic factor in this bound. Sufficient scaling for retaining the clean O(epsilon²) accuracy is p=O(epsilon11). For merely vanishing error, p=o(epsilon9) suffices. These are sufficient estimates, not necessary thresholds or an optimal tolerance claim. Fixed positive density is not controlled asymptotically by this estimate.

## Exact channel telescope

Let A be the exact Heisenberg observable at preparation time, including the final dressing. Enumerate the finite edge set. Since adjoints of CPTP maps are unital completely positive, they contract operator norm. The exact telescope gives

\[
\|\Phi^*(A)-A\|\leq\sum_e p_e\|(\mathcal N_e^*-\mathrm{id})A\|.
\]

Indeed each term is a product of preceding adjoint channels applied to (Phi_e*−id)A. The preceding factors are contractions; the maps on distinct edges commute, but no state factorization is invoked. Define the normalized one-edge Haar twirl E_e, which removes dependence on that edge. The local channel fixes E_e A, so

\[
\|(\mathcal N_e^*-\mathrm{id})A\|
\leq2\|A-E_eA\|
\leq2\sup_{W_e\text{ unitary}}\|[A,W_e]\|.
\]

The last inequality follows by integrating A−W_e A W_e†. The simpler global bound 2||A|| also applies to each local channel difference. Taking expectation in any initial density matrix can only reduce this norm bound.

## Actual fast cone, not a projected-sector cone

The physical Hamiltonian is D+epsilon f V during the ramp and D+epsilon V during the hold. D is a sum of commuting diagonal local star terms. Factoring its evolution changes a local operator only within a fixed star neighborhood: all star terms not meeting its support commute through and cancel. In the interaction picture every native edge term therefore retains a uniformly bounded, finite thickened support and has norm O(epsilon). Time dependence of these terms does not change the standard local Lieb–Robinson estimate. Thus the exact laboratory evolution has a capped exponentially decaying influence bound with speed v<=C epsilon, after a fixed support enlargement.

The final Y14 dressing is generated by a uniformly exponentially local interaction of strength O(epsilon). Its local-observable expansion has summable exponential tails. It adds a bounded cone offset and changes constants, not the epsilon-dependent speed or spatial dimension. Equivalently concatenate the dressing evolution as a bounded fictitious local-time segment.

Consequently, for the Heisenberg A associated with O_X,

\[
\|(\mathcal N_e^*-\mathrm{id})A\|
\leq C_X\|O_X\|\min\{1,\exp[-\mu(d(e,X)-vT_u-c)]\}.
\]

Constants include the fixed star enlargement and dressing. The lattice shell count is at most C(1+r)², also on finite tori when using periodic distance. Split the sum at R=vT_u+c+1. The inside sum is O((1+R)³); the outside sum of (1+r)² exp[−mu(r−R)] is O((1+R)²). Therefore

\[
\sum_e\|(\mathcal N_e^*-\mathrm{id})A\|
\leq C_X\|O_X\|(1+C\epsilon T_u)^3.
\]

This uses the capped exponential directly, rather than choosing a p-dependent hard truncation radius. No log(1/p) loss is required. Multiplying by p proves the noise term. Adding the independently proved clean ramp theorem proves the stated comparison. The proof remains valid with an inert reference and local observables tensored with reference operators of bounded norm, provided the reference has no dynamical couplings or fault channels.

## Why the slow cone has not been substituted

The clean theorem replaces the D-conserving reference generator by a slow extension only after restricting the state to ice. A general N_e can change endpoint charges; Phi(rho0) need not lie in ice, even though p is small locally. The first-order resonant excited-sector terms are not scalar or zero on those sectors. Thus the exact equality used to remove the O(epsilon) interaction fails on error-created charge sectors. The present proof does not justify replacing epsilon T_u by epsilon4 T_u. This is a missing hypothesis for that argument, not a theorem that every improved noise bound is impossible. A sharper estimate would require a separate charged-error propagation analysis or a restrictive ice-preserving noise model.

No thermal preparation, phase, ground-state, fixed-density accuracy or physical occurrence law is supplied by this bound. The product stochastic channel and its rate remain independent preparation assumptions. The canonical clean ramp source is unchanged.
