# Low-pole resolvent geometry and a constructive column cover

New exact analytic consequence of the reviewed native infrared density estimate08471be1 (root independent review eb9afcca). No physical values, Gram, spectrum or integrals evaluated. Units h=1 until the final paragraph. Let H=h0 and v_s=(H-is)^-1e0 for s>0. Its local spectral measure satisfies E f(H²)=E f(X), X=6-2sum cos theta. Put c=1/(4pi) and K=1+1/(12pi²).

## Uniform two-pole estimate without a divided-difference loss

For s,t>=0, s+t>0, define

 M(s,t)=E[1/((X+s²)(X+t²))].

It is finite also when exactly one parameter is zero. Resolvent identity and the spectral theorem give

 ||v_s-v_t||²=(s-t)² M(s,t),
 M(s,t)=[A(s)-A(t)]/(t²-s²) for s!=t.                 (1)

The sign is positive. At s=t>0 the continuous extension is M(s,s)=-A'(s)/(2s). At t=0 use v0=H^-1 e0, which exists because A0<infinity; convergence in norm follows from the same formula below. No bounded operator inverse H^-1 is asserted.

Using x=2sin(theta/2), the density is f0 plus a correction between0 and f0 r²/6 on the unit ball. Extend only the constant-density radial integral to infinity. Elementary partial fractions (with the coincident limit) give

 integral_0^infinity r²/[(r²+s²)(r²+t²)] dr=pi/[2(s+t)].

Consequently its full radial contribution is c/(s+t). The omitted constant-density tail from1 to infinity is at most1/(2pi²), since the integrand is <=r^-2. The density correction on the ball is at most1/(12pi²), since r^4/[(r²+s²)(r²+t²)]<=1. The actual cube exterior contributes between0 and1. Therefore the exact, uniform bound is

 -1/(2pi²) <= M(s,t)-c/(s+t) <= K.                   (2)

In particular

 | ||v_s-v_t||²-c(s-t)²/(s+t) | <= K(s-t)².          (3)

This does not differentiate an unspecified remainder and remains meaningful as t approaches s. It implies the proposed leading coefficient exactly. A lower bound is (s-t)²[c/(s+t)-1/(2pi²)], useful when s+t<pi/2. Zero differences remain exactly zero.

For opposite signs, let v_t^-=(H+it)^-1e0. The same identity gives ||v_s-v_t^-||²=(s+t)²M(s,t), with leading c(s+t) and remainder at most K(s+t)². Thus both signs converge to the same v0. At t=0, ||v_s-v0||²=A0-A(s)=cs+O(s²), consistent with the previous cusp.

## Square-root pole coordinate

Write u=sqrt(s), v=sqrt(t). On0<=s,t<=epsilon, equation(2) yields

 ||v_s-v_t||² <= L_epsilon² |sqrt(s)-sqrt(t)|²,
 L_epsilon²=2c+4K epsilon.                          (4)

Indeed(s-t)²=(u-v)²(u+v)² and(u+v)²<=2(s+t). This also follows from ||d v_(u²)/du||²=4sM(s,s)<=2c+4Ks away from zero; equation(4) includes zero directly. The original parameter s has a derivative norm diverging like s^-1/2, but the square-root coordinate has bounded metric speed. This is not a Taylor expansion of a vector at zero or a lower conditioning theorem.

Choose n intervals of equal length sqrt(epsilon)/n in the u coordinate, with centers at their endpoints s_j=epsilon(j/n)², j=0,...,n. Every s in[0,epsilon] has a center whose squared approximation error is at most

 E_n=L_epsilon² epsilon/(4n²).                      (5)

The same statement holds for each translated site source e_a by magnetic translation covariance of its diagonal spectral measure. For the seven star sites and both pole signs, a subspace generated by these centers has at most7(2n+1) complex generators (the zero center is shared); nearest-center projection error is bounded by E_n for every bare column. This is an existence/selection rule for a pole basis. It requires constructing or representing the inverse-vector centers v0; they are legitimate l2 vectors, not free computational data. One can avoid s0 by using a small positive first center and separately budgeting the zero-end distance. No covariance closure or dynamical invariance follows from these generator counts.

## Actual weighted-operator implication and its premises

Suppose a fixed finite column list uses y_j=sqrt(w_j)v_(s_j,a_j), w_j>=0, total W=sum w_j, and all0<=s_j<=epsilon. Let Pi project onto the center span (enlargement by Gamma0 can only improve residual). Then

 Tr[Y^dagger(I-Pi)Y]<=W E_n,
 Tr(Y^dagger Y)<=W A0.

For Q=Y C Y^dagger with an actually established ||C||<=1, Schatten Cauchy-Schwarz therefore gives

 ||Q-Pi Q Pi||_1 <=2W sqrt(A0 E_n)
                    =W L_epsilon sqrt(A0 epsilon)/n. (6)

Thus n>=W L_epsilon sqrt(A0 epsilon)/eta suffices for this particular operator error eta. This is meaningful only with a certified W and A0 bound and the coefficient contraction premise. It is not obtained by substituting a trace-norm bound for Q in place of W. General Woodbury/finite-quadrature columns may be linear combinations rather than this individually weighted list; their coefficient norms and cancellation-sensitive factor weights must be incorporated before using(6). Bare-column proximity alone does not bound an arbitrary stationary projector or its trace norm.

## Conditioning information that actually follows

For the two-column Gram G_(s,t), testing the unit coefficient vector(1,-1)/sqrt2 gives

 lambda_min(G_(s,t)) <= ||v_s-v_t||²/2
 <= (s-t)²[c/(s+t)+K]/2.                           (7)

If invertible, lambda_max>=TrG/2 gives condition number at least

 [A(s)+A(t)] / {(s-t)²[c/(s+t)+K]}.

Hence close bare poles genuinely produce small Gram eigenvalues, not merely unstable scalar subtraction. This is an upper bound on the smallest eigenvalue and a lower bound on condition number; it supplies no positive lower bound for a many-column Gram. A square-root grid removes needless crowding in the local column metric, but does not prove that its full Gram is well conditioned. Certified rank truncation or suitable confluent/balanced coordinates remain separate tasks.

## Dimensions

For H_h=h H_1, v_s^(h)=h^-1 v_(s/h)^(1). Thus M_h(s,t)=h^-4 M_1(s/h,t/h), its leading term is1/[4pi h³(s+t)], and the remainder is bounded by K/h⁴. The metric leading term is(s-t)²/[4pi h³(s+t)]. All covering formulas can be applied to dimensionless s/h with overall squared-vector factor h^-2. No infinite-volume zero-mode atom is introduced; all limits are taken in the supplied pure Gaussian one-particle representation.
