# Cubic ice correspondence and the induced pure kinetic model

## Disposition

An exact model-family match exists. The cubic degree-three ice carrier has **twenty** local configurations, choose(6,3), not six; “six-vertex” usually identifies the degree-four two-in/two-out model. This terminology matters for searches and citations.

The inspected primary papers support a Coulomb phase in a neighborhood below RK through effective-field-theory arguments, and provide indirect finite-tube evidence near the pure kinetic point. They do not establish a rigorous thermodynamic Coulomb phase at V/J=0, nor locate a certified interval containing exactly .95. No matching source found here establishes crystalline order at V=0 either. This is a bounded five-paper review, not a claim that no later result exists.

## Five primary papers and exact scope

1. **Hermele, Fisher, Balents, “Pyrochlore photons: The U(1) spin liquid in a S=1/2 three-dimensional frustrated magnet,” PRB69,064404 (2004).** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0305401), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.69.064404). Read Sec II B–C, IV B, relevant numerical discussion and Fig1. Despite its title, Sec II B explicitly supplies the matching cubic carrier: spin-half links, three occupied links per vertex, elementary square ring exchange Eq12 and flippability-counting RK potential. Its pyrochlore model instead has tetrahedral constraints and hexagonal rings. Fig1 leaves the lower boundary of the near-RK liquid undetermined. The field theory has quadratic dispersion at RK and linear dispersion for the positive electric-stiffness perturbation below RK. It does not extend this argument all the way to the pure kinetic point. The easy-axis microscopic supplier discussed there is distinct from our native anticommuting-edge perturbation.

2. **Banerjee, Huffman, Rammelmüller, “Exploring bosonic and fermionic link models on (3+1)-D tubes,” PRResearch4,033174 (2022).** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07171), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.033174). Read formulation Eqs4–8/Fig2, methods, spectrum/correlation/flux diagnostics and conclusions. The bosonic/spin-half zero-Gauss model is the relevant match, after link-orientation convention changes; electric E² is constant. Their lambda/J is our V/J. They study narrow periodic tubes, including 2×2×2 and 2×2×4, with larger longitudinal extensions using approximations. Near lambda≈0 they find loss of the large-negative-lambda ordered signatures and indirect liquid evidence. Their conclusion explicitly calls for larger lattices; fixed transverse width is not an isotropic three-dimensional limit. Their fermionic-link variant differs in 3D and is not licensed merely because our original native construction uses CAR: our neutral projected ring dictionary maps to ordinary spin links. No phase result from the fermionic variant should be transferred.

3. **Han and Kivelson, “Emergent gauge fields in band insulators,” PNAS122,e2421778122 (2025).** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17539), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2421778122). Read effective-model and field-theory sections, including the cubic plaquette projectors and Eq3. This supplies another cubic ice setting, obtained from electronic and vibrational degrees of freedom, with three independent diagonal plaquette energies V1,V2,V3 and a ring amplitude J. RK is V3=J,V1=V2=0. The nearby gauge-field description does not determine the complete phase diagram at all diagonal energies zero. Its reference to numerical confirmation on a diamond model is explicitly a different lattice. This is useful prior art for a physical action supplier, but its electrons, phonons and tunneling assumptions are not native axioms or a derived native coupling law.

4. **Moessner and Sondhi, “Three-dimensional resonating-valence-bond liquids and their excitations,” PRB68,184512 (2003).** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307592), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.68.184512). Read model definitions and cubic near-RK discussion. This is the close-packed dimer carrier, with one dimer incident at a site, rather than our three occupied links. The square plaquette algebra looks similar but the local support and background Gauss sector differ. Its cubic Coulomb result is therefore relevant conceptual precedent, not a direct phase certificate for degree-three cubic ice. Similarly, diamond/pyrochlore two-in/two-out ice has coordination four and typically hexagonal minimal rings; it cannot supply a cubic square-ring phase conclusion without additional analysis.

5. **Bravyi, DiVincenzo, Loss, “Schrieffer-Wolff transformation for quantum many-body systems,” Annals of Physics326,2793–2826 (2011).** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0675), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2011.06.004). Read Sec3.1–3.2 and the perturbative tree construction. This is standard prior art for finite-dimensional gapped-block unitary reduction, formal effective-Hamiltonian series and rigorous perturbative control. Their Sec3.1 gap criterion explicitly depends on the perturbation norm. Our Schur elimination and folded normalization are not a new general perturbation method. The potentially new narrow content is the actual native incident-edge sign calculation, support-dependent denominators, orientation/winding bookkeeping and explicit coefficient/remainder in the supplied native model. This review imports no unverified linked-cluster theorem to obtain a volume-uniform bound.

## Operational correspondence and next priority

Our supplied comparison Hamiltonian is H=V sum F_p−J sum flip_p on literal cubic ice. The .95 calculation concerns a near-RK supplied point. The newly induced leading ring model has no nonconstant diagonal potential through the reviewed fourth order (and the separately pending sixth-order calculation); after selecting a compatible kinetic sign convention, its leading ratio is V/J=0. A basis/sign transformation must be written before borrowing the conventional negative off-diagonal Hamiltonian. Extent-four virtual processes also generate winding four-cycles absent from an elementary-plaquette-only comparison. Neither issue can be hidden in the symbol J.

Highest next model question: determine whether the **same carrier and elementary-ring sign convention** remains in the same phase between .95 and0. Before substantial computation, fix the actual effective operator including finite-size winding terms; separate a study of the deliberately supplied plaquette-only model from that of the native finite-volume effective model. A useful discriminator would combine symmetry-breaking observables and winding-sector energies with matched-momentum spectral information, at isotropically increasing sizes. One moment ratio or disappearance of a finite-tube order signature cannot settle a photon pole or bulk phase. Existing near-RK finite estimators cannot simply be relabeled V=0 evidence. No new simulation is authorized or performed by this review.
