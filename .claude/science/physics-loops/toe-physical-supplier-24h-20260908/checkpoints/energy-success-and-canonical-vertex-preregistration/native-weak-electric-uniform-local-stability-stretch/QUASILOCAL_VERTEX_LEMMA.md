# A uniformly bounded one-particle star form factor

This lemma addresses an upper bound, not whether the form factor vanishes at a Dirac node. It uses the supplied local free active Hamiltonians in a fixed canonical pi representative, the actual 90-term star expression, and the uniform wrong-face stiffness. No active gap is assumed. Constants below depend on the fixed ratio of hopping strength to kappa and may be large; they do not depend on volume.

## Smooth inverse filter

Write H=Hpi, H Omega=E0 Omega. For each reversed pair A, H_A=H+B_A with B_A an even quadratic perturbation supported on the center and its neighbors, of bounded norm. The full wrong-flux lower bound gives H_A-E0 >= delta I, with delta=6 kappa valid for every pair (opposite pairs have the stronger 8 kappa bound).

Choose an odd smooth real f on the real line, zero near zero, with f(x)=-1/x for |x|>=delta. It has an inverse Fourier representation f(x)=integral w(t) exp(-itx) dt with integral (1+|t|)^p |w(t)| dt finite for every fixed p. One construction multiplies -1/x by an even smooth cutoff which is zero for |x|<=delta/2 and one for |x|>=delta. To justify the Fourier assertion despite the 1/x tail, subtract the smooth rational function -x/(1+x^2), with the appropriate fixed units: the difference is integrable and smooth, while all positive derivatives of f are integrable. The rational function has a bounded exponentially decaying Fourier transform; repeated integration by parts controls the large-time tail to arbitrary power. Near time zero the transforms are bounded. Denote M_p=integral(1+|t|)^p |w(t)|dt.

Consequently R_A=(E0-H_A)^(-1)=f(H_A-E0) exactly on its entire active spectrum. This does not require any finite-volume upper energy cutoff and does not approximate its eigenvalues.

## An operator local near the star that creates the exact vector

Let U_A(t)=exp(-itH_A)exp(itH). This is a unitary perturbation cocycle. Then

 R_A Omega=Y_A Omega,  Y_A=integral w(t) U_A(t)dt.

For the second inverse and an arbitrary operator X,

 R_C X Omega=integral w(t) U_C(t) tau_t(X)dt Omega,
 tau_t(X)=exp(-itH)X exp(itH).

It follows exactly that chi_v=Y_v Omega, where

 Y_v=(1/8) sum_{A,C disjoint} integral ds dt w(s)w(t)
             U_C(t) tau_t(gamma_v U_A(s)).

The sum has 90 terms. Y_v is odd and need not be Hermitian; this is a vacuum-creating representative, not equality Y_v=O_v on the whole active space. Its norm is at most (90/8) M_0^2, uniformly in volume.

Moreover Y_v admits approximants supported in radius r about v with error at most C_p(1+r)^(-p), for every fixed p, uniformly in volume. Here is the locality argument. Finite-range bounded quadratic CAR evolution has the usual propagation estimate obtained by summing hopping paths: the tail of exp(-itK) beyond graph distance r is bounded by a constant times exp(v|t|-mu r), for fixed positive mu and a volume-independent v. This gives the corresponding bound for evolved local even perturbations and local odd operators. Duhamel's equation for U_A(t), generated by the evolved B_A, bounds its error upon restriction to a ball by a polynomial in |t| times exp(v|t|-mu r). For the product above, use balls with fixed fractional radii and split the integrals into |s|+|t|<=c r and its complement. On the first part the propagation tail is exponentially small, after choosing c sufficiently small; on the second part arbitrarily high moments M_p give arbitrary inverse powers of r. Operator norms of the exact unitary factors are one. The finitely many factors and the 90-term sum do not create a volume factor. The argument works on finite tori using their graph metric, with the ball eventually equal to the whole torus. No long-time exponential-decay claim for the gapless vacuum is used.

## Gaussian extraction and a uniform Fourier bound

For any finite-support odd active operator Y in a Gaussian vacuum, its one-particle vacuum component equals that of sum_j c_j gamma_j, where

 c_j=(1/2)<Omega|{gamma_j,Y}|Omega>.

This is Wick's rule: in an odd Majorana monomial the one surviving leg can be extracted by the anticommutator, leaving the fully contracted even monomial. It extends linearly to every local operator, and then continuously to the quasi-local approximants. Hermiticity of Y is unnecessary; the coefficients may be complex and need not be unique after acting on the vacuum. Outside the support the anticommutator vanishes by graded locality, while |c_j|<=||Y||.

Decompose Y_v into dyadic support shells using approximants at radii 2^n. A shell has norm at most constant*2^(-np) and contains at most constant*2^(3n) sites. With p>3, its coefficient l1 norm is summable. Thus there is a representation of P1 chi_v as (sum_j c_vj gamma_j)Omega with

 sum_j |c_vj| <= C(kappa,h),

uniformly in volume. Magnetic translations give the corresponding translated/gauged coefficient family, so its cell Fourier symbol is uniformly bounded. This proves the bounded-form-factor premise needed in the preceding supplement. It neither supplies a positive lower bound nor proves a zero at the nodes.

## Consequence and exact remaining gap

For the canonical antiperiodic cubic grids, the one-particle singleton resolvent kernel has a uniformly bounded row l2 norm, because its Fourier symbol is bounded by constant/omega(k) and the normalized sum of omega(k)^(-2) is bounded in three dimensions. The shell counting is sum_{j<=O(L)} L^(-3)(j+1)^2 L^2/(j+1)^2=O(1). Together with CAR this bounds the norm of each corresponding linear spectator field and hence the local commutator of the quadratic singleton contribution, without replacing l2 by a divergent l1 bound.

This is not the full sixth effective operator or a proof of interacting local joint-defect stability. It controls a particular vacuum transition and its one-particle singleton channel. Higher odd transition sectors, mixed histories, operator-valued connected cancellations and uniform control of the interacting reference still require proof. The construction is useful precisely because it does not assume the uniform active gap that would trivially, but incorrectly for this problem, solve the infrared denominator issue.
