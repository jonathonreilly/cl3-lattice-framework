# Independent hop-only D2 connectivity argument

Premises: the full low-charge edge configuration domain on C_L1 square C_L2 square C_L3, each extent even and >=4, with exactly one positive and one negative signed charge. No fixed winding or cycle constraint. Previously independently proved position reachability is imported: either charge can reach any vertex other than the fixed opposite charge through legal hops. Electric arrows have outdegree3+Q. The argument below concerns configuration support; nonzero native phases are retained but do not affect edge existence.

## Every directed simple cycle can be reversed with positions restored

Induct on cycle length in the current orientation, at the current signed charge positions. If a cycle avoids the negative charge, use the positive charge: choose a directed access path avoiding the negative, stopped at its first cycle vertex. It is internally disjoint from the cycle. Access, one complete forward traversal, then return along reversed access toggles only cycle edges overall. If the cycle instead avoids the positive, use the negative charge with arrows reversed. Both operations preserve the other charge and restore all off-cycle edges.

It remains to treat a cycle containing both charges. First suppose it has no chord in the underlying undirected graph. The positive vertex has four outgoing edges. Exactly one is its outgoing cycle edge; the other three cannot terminate elsewhere on the induced cycle. All three terminate at neutral vertices outside it. Park the positive charge along any one such edge. This changes no cycle edge. The negative charge now traverses the complete cycle against its arrows, avoiding the parked positive. It returns to its original position and reverses exactly the cycle. The positive then returns along the reversed parking edge. That edge was not touched by the traversal; the original positive site is neutral at return, so the return hop is legal. The net operation reverses precisely the cycle and restores both positions.

If the cycle has a chord joining nonconsecutive vertices u,v, its orientation is either u->v or v->u. Name endpoints so it is u->v. The original cycle has directed arcs P:u->v and Q:v->u. First reverse the shorter directed cycle consisting of chord u->v followed by Q. Its length is strictly less than the original because both original arcs have at least two edges. The chord now points v->u, while P remains unchanged. Reverse the shorter directed cycle P followed by that reversed chord. Both subcycle reversals are available by induction with charge positions restored after each; intermediate orientations remain in D2. The chord is flipped twice and restored, while both original arcs are reversed. No assumption that either subcycle avoids charges is needed: that is exactly the induction hypothesis. On this bipartite simple graph subcycles are also legitimate even cycles, with no parallel-edge exception.

The base case is any chordless cycle, already handled. This proves the statement for every directed simple cycle. It uses a finite sequence, not a quantitative length/mixing bound.

## Equal positions imply configuration connectivity

For two electric orientations with identical Q, retain the edges on which they differ and orient them as in the first configuration. At every vertex the changed outgoing count equals the changed incoming count: total divergence agrees in the two orientations, and reversing a changed edge negates its contribution. Hence this finite balanced directed subgraph decomposes into edge-disjoint directed simple cycles. Reverse those cycles one at a time using the preceding construction. Completed cycles change no remaining difference edge, so each next cycle is still directed. The resulting electric configuration is exactly the requested second configuration and the signed positions are restored throughout each operation.

For configurations with different positions, first use the earlier position theorem to move the signed pair to the target positions (at most three path operations, with a spare vertex if needed). Then apply the equal-position argument. Thus the entire D2 hopping-support graph is connected. This includes changes of winding because all configurations are admitted; it is not a theorem inside an independently imposed winding subspace.

## Finite spectral consequence

In the independently checked signed-hole basis every D2 native hopping entry is -1, while every gated ring entry is positive before the Hamiltonian minus sign. For t>0 and nonnegative possibly nonuniform Jp, H has real nonpositive off-diagonal entries and a connected off-diagonal support, because hopping alone connects it. Shift cI-H with c sufficiently large to make every entry nonnegative. It is irreducible; finite Perron–Frobenius gives a simple maximal eigenvalue and a strictly positive eigenvector. Equivalently H has a unique D2 ground eigenvector up to phase, positive in this basis. For t<0 use epsilon_x epsilon_y to reverse all hop signs while preserving rings. Thus uniqueness holds for every t!=0 on the finite stated D2 domain, independently of U (which is the constant2U here). No uniqueness is inferred at t=0 or for the global all-D ground.

This does not prove a volume-uniform gap, spectral rate, quantum transport speed, equilibration, physical preparation, deconfinement or a selected Hamiltonian. Perron positivity is a basis statement after the exact dictionary and supplied signed-hole frame, not native phase cancellation by assumption. Additional fixed-code or winding restrictions change the configuration graph and are outside the proof.
