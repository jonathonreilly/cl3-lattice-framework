The following proof applies to the entire complete-graph at-most-two-site family; hence also to the fixed NN line and exactly-weight-two subsets. It does not use the earlier encoding Hamming-distance obstruction or endpoint matrix elements as a reachability substitute.

Use the Y eigenbasis on x and computational bases on f,b0,b1. This basis change preserves operator weight and allowed supports and is purely mathematical. Let y=±1, q=0,1, b0,b1=0,1. Apart from the constant1/2, the exact diagonal energy is
 k(y,q,b0,b1)=q(1+y)+b0+2b1.
Every energy-commuting Hermitian H has H_ab=0 whenever k(a) differs from k(b). We must apply this to the SUM of its Pauli terms, not to individual terms.

Group matrix elements by their set of flipped qubits in this product basis. An at-most-two-site operator has no flip set larger than two. For a two-qubit flip set, its matrix coefficient can depend on the input values of those two qubits but cannot depend on any other spectator: a term doing so would have weight at least three. Different flip sets contribute to different matrix elements and cannot cancel one another. Single-flip coefficients may contain conditional diagonal terms on other sites; the argument below allows these sums.

First consider any transition flipping b0. A lone b0 flip changes energy by±1. If its only second flipped qubit is x, the added gap is0 or±2; if f, it is0 or±2; if b1, it is±2; if an energy-free head/label/sentinel qubit, it is0. None cancels±1. This remains true for all spectator configurations, so every matrix element flipping b0 vanishes. Thus [H,n_b0]=0 globally.

Next consider a transition flipping b1. Alone, or together with an energy-free qubit, its gap is±2 and is nonzero. Together with b0 the gap is±2±1 and is nonzero. Together with x, the q=0 spectator sector has gap±2 regardless of x, so all such two-site flip coefficients vanish there. They are independent of spectator q by their support bound, and therefore vanish also in q=1, even when that latter sector happens to be resonant. Together with f, the y=-1 spectator sector likewise has gap±2. The two-site coefficient cannot depend on spectator x/y, so it vanishes also at y=+1. Hence every b1-flipping matrix element vanishes and [H,n_b1]=0 globally. A sum of allowed two-site terms cannot supply the missing third-site conditioning.

It follows from [H,K]=0 and both battery invariants that
 [H,Qactive]=0, Qactive=q(I+Yx).
The spectral projector Pbright=Qactive/2 onto the charged Y+ state is an exact common invariant of the WHOLE allowed Hamiltonian family. The source has Pbright=1; target has Pbright=0. Every finite concatenation of admissible exponentials, every generated Lie operator, and every well-defined time-dependent schedule of these controls preserves Pbright. Thus source-to-target transition probability is zero. This is a genuine energy/control-support obstruction for this supplied fixture after arbitrary intermediate workspace states are admitted. The argument is global on the nine-qubit Hilbert space, not merely on the prepared legal sector, and needs neither [H,Zr] nor [H,Nhead]; adding those constraints cannot remove the obstruction.

Crucially fuel itself is NOT invariant for the at-most-two family. Hdark=Xf(I-Yx) is an allowed sum of one- and two-site terms, with adjacent support x-f on the fixed line. Since (I-Yx)(I+Yx)=0, it commutes with K exactly, and it trivially preserves the old sentinel and head number; it does not commute with q. This explicit example rules out a false stronger fuel-invariance argument and demonstrates why solving constraints separately term-by-term would be incorrect. It couples only the zero-active-energy sector, not the charged bright source.

This proof identifies invariants rather than claiming a full enumeration of commutant ranks or Lie dimensions. Primary's independent exact nullspace computation can corroborate the invariant basiswise. Any reported violation by a returned basis vector would flag an implementation issue or a changed control premise and must be resolved rather than ignored.

Scope: this is not a universal energy-conserving-control no-go. The separation depends on the specified q(1+y) interaction, binary battery weights1 and2, and a maximum two-site support with no additional energetic degrees of freedom or nonconserving drives. Three-site conditional resonant exchanges can evade the missing-spectator argument, and supplied extra controls would require their own separately scoped attempt.
