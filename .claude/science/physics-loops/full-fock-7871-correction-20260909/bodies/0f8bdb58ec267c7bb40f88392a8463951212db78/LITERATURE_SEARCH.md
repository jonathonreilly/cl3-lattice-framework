# Literature proof search: foundations review of a discrete-record framework

Search date: 2026-08-01. Scope: theorem- and argument-level comparison, not endorsement of any interpretation. “E1” below means record formation at first admissibility; “E2” means formation at first orbit-admissibility/clean recurrence. The toy-model census and covariance results are treated as internal givens, not as claims established by the cited literature.

## Records, objectivity, and deterministic ontology

Source | Wojciech H. Zurek, “Quantum Darwinism,” *Nature Physics* 5, 181–188 (2009).
Problem it solves | Explains the operational objectivity and classical robustness of selected quantum observables by the proliferation of many independently accessible environmental imprints.
Premises | A system–environment tensor-product split; unitary system–environment interaction; decoherence/einselection of stable pointer states; observers access fragments of the environment; quantum mutual information (and, in sharper formulations, Holevo information/discord); an initial state and Hamiltonian.
Proof skeleton | Compute reduced states after system–environment correlation; identify pointer observables stable under the interaction; plot information about the system available in an environment fraction; a redundancy plateau means many disjoint fragments each reveal nearly all classical information about the pointer observable, supporting intersubjective agreement without direct system access.
What maps to this framework | The closest analogue of an “absolute” record is an imprint recoverable by many differently placed, non-disturbing monitors from disjoint carriers. It supplies a quantitative robustness test stronger than mere covariance of a record with its fixture. The monitor-relative reading resembles a fragment whose correlations reveal only a measurement-context-dependent observable and fail redundant broadcast.
What does not map | Quantum Darwinism does not postulate permanent, write-once, one-record-per-site events; its records are correlations in a unitary global state and can be recohered in principle. It does not select a single actual outcome. Redundancy, system/environment factorization, entropy, probabilities, and observer access channels are absent from the four axioms.
Runner/proof translation candidate | On the finite runner, define a family of transformed, disjoint monitor fixtures and a record variable for each; prove whether the same underlying site event can be decoded with error zero from `R` disjoint fixtures. Compare `R > 1` fixture-independent decoding (“absolute”) with `R = 1` or basis/fixture-dependent decoding. This is a finite combinatorial surrogate for redundancy, not quantum mutual information.
Import risk | High if “absolute” is identified with Darwinian objectivity without adding independent copies and access maps; medium if used only as an adversarial test. Covariance under jointly transforming the apparatus is weaker than redundant, apparatus-independent availability.
Citation to preserve | [Zurek, *Nature Physics* 5, 181–188 (2009), doi:10.1038/nphys1202](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys1202); open preprint context: [arXiv:0707.2832](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2832).

Source | Gerard ’t Hooft, *The Cellular Automaton Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics* (Springer, 2016; preprint begun 2014).
Problem it solves | Proposes a deterministic ontology underneath quantum mechanics, with Hilbert-space methods used as a calculational representation of classical automaton states, aiming to remove fundamental collapse and measurement indeterminism.
Premises | A deterministic (sometimes information-losing) cellular automaton; an ontological basis whose basis vectors represent mutually exclusive states; deterministic evolution permutes ontological states in the reversible case; additional equivalence classes may be used under information loss; strong constraints concerning Bell correlations and measurement-setting independence/superdeterminism.
Proof skeleton | Embed classical configurations as an orthonormal basis; represent deterministic time evolution by a permutation matrix, hence a unitary operator when invertible; change basis to obtain superpositions and quantum-style operators; argue that observables called beables are diagonal in the ontological basis and that quantum formalism can describe the deterministic substrate.
What maps to this framework | The 11-site supplied deterministic update can be represented by permutation dynamics on basis states if it is bijective. “Only records are readable” is analogous, at most, to privileging beables/ontological observables. Both approaches sharply separate substrate/update content from the Hilbert-space description.
What does not map | The reviewed framework begins with local `M_2(C)` possibility domains but does not identify a global ontological basis, impose permutation/unitary evolution, solve Bell constraints, or derive quantum statistics. A permanently locked record is not an ontological state in ’t Hooft’s sense, and calling records “reality” would add an ontology not entailed by “only records are readable.”
Runner/proof translation candidate | If the runner update is bijective, construct its permutation matrix `U_f|x⟩ = |f(x)⟩`; prove exactly which record predicates are diagonal and invariant/covariant under the translation action. If it is non-bijective, explicitly compute equivalence classes of states with identical futures before invoking an information-loss construction.
Import risk | Very high as physical support: the interpretation is a speculative completion with controversial superdeterministic commitments. Low as a finite mathematical representation of a reversible deterministic update.
Citation to preserve | [’t Hooft, arXiv:1405.1548](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.1548); [Springer book, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-41285-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-41285-6).

## Quantum cellular automata and the supplied local rule

Source | Benjamin Schumacher and Reinhard F. Werner, “Reversible quantum cellular automata” (2004).
Problem it solves | Gives an operator-algebraic definition of QCA and characterizes when a translation-covariant local rule induces a causal, reversible global quantum evolution.
Premises | An infinite lattice of finite-dimensional cell algebras; a global *-automorphism commuting with translations; strict finite propagation; in the main nearest-neighbor structure theorem, matrix cell algebras and reversible quantum dynamics.
Proof skeleton | Express causality as inclusions of local observable algebras; use commutants and finite-dimensional factor decompositions to identify mutually commuting “support” subalgebras; decompose the global automorphism into a generalized Margolus scheme of blockwise unitaries; infer that the inverse of a nearest-neighbor QCA is again local.
What maps to this framework | Same broad ingredients: lattice sites, identical finite matrix algebras, translation covariance, and finite neighborhoods. It supplies a rigorous consistency test if the downstream rule is claimed to be a reversible Heisenberg-picture quantum dynamics.
What does not map | An admissibility predicate or classical deterministic update is not a *-automorphism. The framework does not assume global unitarity, causality in the operator-algebraic sense, reversibility, or a tensor-product quasi-local algebra. Proper cubic-rotation covariance adds symmetry but does not supply these missing hypotheses.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Formalize the supplied rule as a candidate map on local observables, then mechanically test unitality, multiplicativity, *-preservation, translation commutation, and the causal cone. Only if these pass should the support-algebra/Margolus decomposition be translated.
Import risk | High if used to say all allowed framework dynamics must be unitary or partitioned; low if used conditionally: “any downstream rule advertised as a reversible QCA must satisfy these tests.” The original full structure claim is dimension-sensitive in its formulation and should not be paraphrased as a universal two-layer circuit theorem without checking the version.
Citation to preserve | [Schumacher and Werner, arXiv:quant-ph/0405174](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0405174).

Source | Pablo Arrighi, “An overview of Quantum Cellular Automata,” *Natural Computing* 18, 885–899 (2019; living arXiv version updated subsequently).
Problem it solves | Organizes equivalent/related QCA definitions, localizability and partitioned-QCA structure results, universality, and discrete models of quantum fields.
Premises | Arrays of identical finite-dimensional quantum systems; a discrete-time unitary global evolution; bounded-speed causality; translation invariance; encodings and coarse-grainings when discussing intrinsic simulation.
Proof skeleton | Derive local implementability from unitarity plus causality, generally after adding local ancillas and arranging finite layers; show partitioned QCA can intrinsically simulate general QCA; build universal scattering rules and recover example continuum dynamics through controlled limits.
What maps to this framework | Offers a menu of downstream rules compatible with discrete space, finite local dimension, bounded propagation, and homogeneity. The 3D one-qubit-per-cell constructions show those kinematic choices are not by themselves empty.
What does not map | Existence or universality of a QCA does not choose the actual rule, record-formation trigger, initial condition, Born weights, or continuum limit. Partition clocks/blockings can introduce structure that must be shown compatible with full lattice translations and proper cubic rotations rather than assumed.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Encode the toy rule as a partitioned automaton and prove an intertwining relation `E f = G^k E` for an explicit encoding `E`. Separately prove the record predicate commutes with translations/rotations including the monitoring fixture. This would distinguish dynamical simulation from record covariance.
Import risk | Medium: this is a review, so use it to locate theorem hypotheses, not as a substitute for them. Universality is an existence/simulation property and gives no uniqueness or empirical-selection argument for the supplied rule.
Citation to preserve | [Arrighi, arXiv:1904.12956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12956); [*Natural Computing* 18, 885–899, doi:10.1007/s11047-019-09762-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11047-019-09762-6).

Source | Terry Farrelly, “A review of Quantum Cellular Automata,” *Quantum* 4, 368 (2020).
Problem it solves | Reviews QCA structure, 1D index theory, higher-dimensional classification issues, quantum computation, Floquet phases, and lattice discretizations of quantum field theory.
Premises | Local finite-dimensional quantum systems (or carefully defined fermionic analogues); global locality-preserving unitary evolution/automorphism; strict causal range; often translation invariance, though parts of modern QCA theory relax it.
Proof skeleton | Relate causal automorphisms to finite-depth local circuits plus genuinely transport-like QCA components; in one dimension assign an index measuring net quantum information flow and prove stability/multiplicativity; survey how symmetries and dimension obstruct simple classifications.
What maps to this framework | The index perspective suggests a sharp invariant for any reversible downstream rule, and the survey clarifies that locality plus homogeneity leaves many inequivalent dynamics. That reinforces—rather than fills—the framework’s explicit “supplied rule” slot.
What does not map | The framework’s write-once record operation is generically nonunitary if it overwrites a qubit and therefore is not itself a QCA automorphism. Three-dimensional QCA classification is substantially richer and does not collapse to the 1D toy result. QFT continuum recovery requires scaling and symmetry assumptions not in the axioms.
Runner/proof translation candidate | For a reversible 1D supplied rule, calculate a classical information-flow analogue (or the Gross–Nesme–Vogts–Werner index if genuinely quantum) and test invariance under local circuit changes. Use it to show that two covariant rules can be dynamically inequivalent despite identical record syntax.
Import risk | Medium-low as a boundary map; high if a 1D index or circuit decomposition is exported to the 3D cubic setting without proof. Modern QCA results constrain a specified global evolution, not a local menu of “admissible possibilities.”
Citation to preserve | [Farrelly, *Quantum* 4, 368 (2020), doi:10.22331/q-2020-11-30-368](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2020-11-30-368); [arXiv:1904.13318](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.13318).

## Consistent/decoherent histories and the E1/E2 event fork

Source | Robert B. Griffiths, “Consistent histories and the interpretation of quantum mechanics,” *Journal of Statistical Physics* 36, 219–272 (1984).
Problem it solves | Extends quantum reasoning to sequences of properties at multiple times while retaining ordinary probability rules inside suitably consistent families, without making measurement fundamental.
Premises | Hilbert space, initial state, unitary time evolution, time-ordered projector decompositions, class/chain operators, and a consistency (decoherence) condition that removes interference between alternative histories; logical reasoning is confined to one compatible framework.
Proof skeleton | Represent a history by a temporal string of projectors; assign candidate weights using chain operators; impose vanishing cross terms so coarse-grained alternatives obey Kolmogorov sum rules; reason probabilistically within that consistent family and prohibit combining incompatible families.
What maps to this framework | E1 and E2 can be treated as two different temporal coarse-grainings/event algebras: E1 asks for the first local hit, E2 for the first hit satisfying a trajectory-orbit predicate. The literature makes explicit that choosing an event family is substantive, not merely notation.
What does not map | The reviewed framework has no Hilbert-space history state, unitary class operators, decoherence functional, or probability measure. Permanent records are single actual locks, not projectors in multiple alternative histories. Consistency alone would not decide E1 versus E2.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Construct Boolean cylinder events for the finite deterministic trajectories and their chain indicators. Prove E1 and E2 generate distinct event partitions; calculate their common refinement and exhibit propositions that cannot be identified equivariantly. This imports the histories bookkeeping, not quantum consistency probabilities.
Import risk | Medium: the analogy is useful for exposing a realm/event-selection problem, but quantum incompatibility is not established by two differing classical stopping-time definitions.
Citation to preserve | [Griffiths, *J. Stat. Phys.* 36, 219–272 (1984), doi:10.1007/BF01015734](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01015734).

Source | Murray Gell-Mann and James B. Hartle, “Quantum Mechanics in the Light of Quantum Cosmology,” in *Complexity, Entropy, and the Physics of Information*, ed. W. H. Zurek (Addison-Wesley, 1990), pp. 425–458.
Problem it solves | Formulates decoherent-histories quantum mechanics for a closed universe and seeks quasiclassical domains whose coarse-grained histories decohere and display persistent classical correlations.
Premises | An initial quantum state of the universe; Hamiltonian/dynamics; exhaustive coarse-grained histories; a decoherence functional; approximate vanishing of interference; further criteria such as maximal refinement and classical predictability for quasiclassical realms.
Proof skeleton | Form class operators for coarse-grained histories; evaluate the decoherence functional; when off-diagonal terms are negligible, assign diagonal probabilities; search among decohering coarse grainings for domains with approximately classical correlations and records.
What maps to this framework | E1 versus E2 resembles a choice of coarse graining that changes which temporal propositions count as events. The need to specify initial condition, dynamics, and a selection criterion parallels the framework’s honest placement of these items downstream.
What does not map | Decoherence is approximate and probabilistic; framework locks are exact and permanent. A decohering realm contains alternative histories, whereas the record axiom asserts an actual readable event without providing amplitudes or a realm-selection principle. “Records form” is not a decoherence functional.
Runner/proof translation candidate | For every initial condition in the closed-form census, construct E1 and E2 trajectory partitions; order them by refinement; test whether either maximizes a precisely chosen classicality score invariant under `C_11`. A failure theorem would be the finite analogue of realm non-selection, not a quantum-cosmology result.
Import risk | High if “decoherence selects records” is asserted: the framework lacks the required state, dynamics, environment/coarse graining, and probability rule. Medium as a template for separating consistency from selection.
Citation to preserve | [Gell-Mann and Hartle, arXiv:1803.04605 (archival posting of the 1990 chapter)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04605); [CaltechAUTHORS record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/91z2r-vc775).

Source | Roland Omnès, “Consistent Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics,” *Reviews of Modern Physics* 64, 339–382 (1992).
Problem it solves | Systematizes consistent histories as a logical framework for quantum properties at multiple times and connects it to decoherence, classical phenomenology, measurement records, and factual reasoning.
Premises | Universal quantum mechanics; density operator and Hamiltonian; sequences of projectors/properties; consistency conditions permitting probability additivity and ordinary logic within a family; coarse graining and decoherence for macroscopic/quasiclassical descriptions.
Proof skeleton | Define histories and their chain operators; impose consistency so interference does not spoil additivity; build Boolean-style logical implication inside each consistent family; use coarse-grained collective observables and environmental decoherence to establish approximate classical determinism and support inferences backward from records.
What maps to this framework | Omnès makes the “field of propositions” explicit. E1 and E2 therefore should be formalized as different temporal proposition fields, with a proof of what implications each allows from a present record to a past event. His distinction between a factual datum and probabilistic theory is close to the framework’s emphasis on readable records.
What does not map | The logical rules are licensed by quantum consistency/decoherence and probabilities, none of which follows from permanent locks. Approximate macroscopic facts are not exact sitewise record axioms. A present record does not by itself justify a unique retrodiction without dynamics and a chosen consistent family.
Runner/proof translation candidate | For each reading, define the Boolean algebra generated by time-indexed clean predicates and record predicates. Compute implication `R_t ⇒ E_s` over the entire initial-condition census, list which retrodictions are sound, and prove whether the E1 and E2 logics admit a common nontrivial refinement.
Import risk | Medium: the formal “logic of histories” is a useful proof template, but identifying classical consistency of deterministic predicates with quantum decoherence would erase the core physical premise.
Citation to preserve | [Omnès, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 64, 339 (1992), doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.64.339](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.64.339).

Source | Fay Dowker and Adrian Kent, “Properties of Consistent Histories,” *Physical Review Letters* 75, 3038–3041 (1995).
Problem it solves | Tests whether the consistency criterion alone recovers standard quasiclassical predictions, retrodictions, and inferences, and supplies counterexamples showing that it does not.
Premises | The Gell-Mann–Hartle histories formalism, its consistency/decoherence criteria, and freedom to choose among multiple consistent sets.
Proof skeleton | Construct mutually incompatible consistent extensions containing contrary or otherwise nonclassical retrodictions; show that each passes consistency although their inferences cannot be combined; conclude that consistency alone is insufficient to single out the physically intended quasiclassical reasoning.
What maps to this framework | This is the sharpest literature warning for the E1/E2 fork: demonstrating that each record rule is covariant and internally clean would not select one as physically real. A further, non-question-begging event-selection/formation principle or empirical discriminator is required.
What does not map | The toy model’s two deterministic readings are not automatically contrary quantum inferences, and their coexistence does not itself reproduce the Dowker–Kent examples. The criticism targets probabilistic consistent sets, not classical stopping rules.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Exhaustively enumerate every covariant clean-event predicate in the 11-site model, quotient by logical equivalence, and exhibit at least two inequivalent predicates satisfying all stated axioms but disagreeing on some orbit. Then prove no invariant built only from the four axioms distinguishes them. This is a direct finite non-uniqueness theorem.
Import risk | Low as an objection schema; high if the historical result is claimed to prove the framework inconsistent. It proves underdetermination of selection under its own premises, suggesting—but not establishing—the analogous finite theorem.
Citation to preserve | [Dowker and Kent, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 75, 3038 (1995), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.3038](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.3038); [arXiv:gr-qc/9409037](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9409037).

## Born-rule derivations and no-free-probability warnings

Source | Andrew M. Gleason, “Measures on the Closed Subspaces of a Hilbert Space,” *Journal of Mathematics and Mechanics* 6, 885–893 (1957).
Problem it solves | Characterizes normalized, additive probability measures on all projectors/closed subspaces of a Hilbert space and shows that, in dimension greater than two, they have density-operator (Born-rule) form.
Premises | A real or complex Hilbert space of dimension at least three; a nonnegative measure on every closed subspace/projector; countable additivity on mutually orthogonal alternatives; normalization. In modern language the probability assignment is noncontextual: the value for a projector does not depend on the other projectors measured alongside it.
Proof skeleton | Convert the measure to a frame function on unit vectors whose values sum to a constant on every orthonormal basis; prove sufficiently constrained frame functions are quadratic forms; represent the quadratic form by a positive trace-one operator ρ, giving μ(P)=Tr(ρP).
What maps to this framework | The axiom that scalar readout is additive over disjoint records is superficially measure-like. Gleason provides an exact checklist showing what more would be needed before quadratic weights follow.
What does not map | A local `M_2(C)` is two-dimensional, so the original theorem does not constrain probability assignments on its rank-one projectors. “Disjoint records” are spatially disjoint actual objects, not necessarily orthogonal projectors forming every measurement context. No normalized probability measure on possibilities is supplied. A larger global Hilbert space would help with dimension but would still require a global state, projector event algebra, additivity, and noncontextuality.
Runner/proof translation candidate | First prove or refute a representation theorem for the actual readout functional: specify its domain, zero and normalization, and whether spatial additivity plus covariance forces only a counting measure `S(R) = c|R|`. Then demonstrate by counterexample that this does not determine probabilities over which records form.
Import risk | Very high if cited as deriving Born weights from `M_2` plus additivity. The dimension-two exception and the change from orthogonal alternatives to disjoint realized records are decisive.
Citation to preserve | [Gleason, *J. Math. Mech.* 6, 885–893 (1957), doi:10.1512/iumj.1957.6.56050](https://doi.org/10.1512/iumj.1957.6.56050).

Source | Paul Busch, “Quantum States and Generalized Observables: A Simple Proof of Gleason’s Theorem,” *Physical Review Letters* 91, 120403 (2003).
Problem it solves | Extends a Gleason-type density-operator representation to normalized generalized probability assignments on quantum effects/POVM elements, including the qubit case.
Premises | A function on the full effect algebra `0 ≤ E ≤ I`; normalization; additivity whenever effects sum to an allowed effect (equivalently consistent probability assignments across POVMs); positivity and mild regularity consequences.
Proof skeleton | Use effect additivity to obtain rational and then real homogeneity; extend the assignment to a positive linear functional on bounded operators; apply finite-dimensional representation to obtain `v(E) = Tr(ρE)`.
What maps to this framework | Because each site has `M_2`, this is the mathematically relevant repair of the original Gleason dimension gap—if “local possibilities” are explicitly identified with all effects and supplied with a normalized noncontextual probability assignment.
What does not map | The four axioms supply neither POVMs nor a probability value for every effect. Additivity of scalar readouts over already formed, spatially disjoint records is not POVM additivity over unrealized alternative effects. Thus the theorem relocates rather than eliminates the main probabilistic premise.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Encode the framework’s admissible local possibilities and test closure under effect sums and convex scaling. If closure fails, produce a formal non-applicability lemma. If it succeeds only after completion to the full effect algebra, list that completion and normalized effect-measure assumption as explicit downstream imports.
Import risk | High: “works for qubits” can conceal the stronger effect/POVM noncontextuality premise. Valuable chiefly as a precise boundary theorem.
Citation to preserve | [Busch, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 91, 120403 (2003), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.120403](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.120403); [arXiv:quant-ph/9909073](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9909073).

Source | David Deutsch, “Quantum Theory of Probability and Decisions,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 455, 3129–3137 (1999).
Problem it solves | Attempts to recover the practical probability rule of quantum theory from non-probabilistic quantum mechanics plus decision theory by constraining rational values assigned to quantum games.
Premises | Unitary quantum mechanics interpreted through quantum games with specified observables and payoffs; an agent’s value function; decision-theoretic consistency, substitutability/equivalence, additivity/zero-sum structure, and sufficient availability of games. Measurement neutrality or an equivalent implementation-indifference premise is implicit in later reconstructions.
Proof skeleton | Use symmetries to equate values of equal-amplitude outcomes; transform games without changing their rational value; use compound/fine-grained games for rational amplitude-squared ratios; extend to general amplitudes and obtain an expected-utility expression weighted by squared amplitudes.
What maps to this framework | It shows the precise sort of bridge required to turn deterministic structure into action-guiding weights: define alternatives, rewards, agents, and equivalences among implementations. None follows merely from spatial covariance.
What does not map | The framework provides actual locked records rather than a menu of quantum games, and it supplies no amplitudes, preferences, utilities, agents, or rationality postulates. A value rule for bets would not specify which record forms or its objective formation RATE.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Define finite bets over record histories and list the exact symmetry and substitution axioms on their values. Derive any representation theorem conditionally, then construct alternative value functions when one axiom is removed to expose where a probability-like weight enters.
Import risk | Very high. The proof’s interpretation and implicit neutrality assumptions prompted substantial debate; its conclusion cannot be detached from the quantum-game and rationality premises.
Citation to preserve | [Deutsch, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 455, 3129–3137 (1999), doi:10.1098/rspa.1999.0443](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1999.0443); [arXiv:quant-ph/9906015](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9906015).

Source | David Wallace, “How to Prove the Born Rule,” in *Many Worlds? Everett, Quantum Theory, and Reality* (Oxford University Press, 2010); preprint title “A formal proof of the Born rule from decision-theoretic assumptions” (2009).
Problem it solves | Makes the Deutsch program explicit and mathematically rigorous: a rational agent in an Everettian quantum universe must evaluate available acts using squared-amplitude branch weights.
Premises | Universal unitary/Everettian quantum mechanics and branching; quantum acts with rewards; a total preference ordering; richness/availability of acts; ordering, diachronic consistency, macrostate indifference, branching indifference, state supervenience, and solution continuity.
Proof skeleton | Prove an Everettian representation theorem from the preference axioms; establish equivalence of acts related by branch-state symmetries; derive equal weighting of equal-amplitude branches; use fine-graining and continuity to show that preferences maximize expected utility with weights equal to squared amplitudes.
What maps to this framework | Its value lies in premise bookkeeping: if a records theory seeks decision weights, it must state the agent/act/utility layer and prove a representation result rather than relabel record additivity as probability.
What does not map | The framework has one actual locked record per site, not coexisting Everett branches. It contains no universal state, branching ontology, amplitudes, agents, preferences, rewards, or availability axioms. The theorem concerns rational action and explicitly does not by itself settle all questions of chance, evidence, or observed frequency.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Formalize bets on the deterministic toy model only after defining an ignorance distribution or branch structure. Prove a representation theorem from stated preference axioms, label the result as a decision weight, and exhibit models with the same record axioms but different priors to prove non-derivability.
Import risk | Very high. Critics challenge whether branching indifference, state supervenience, and related axioms are independently compelling or encode the amplitude measure; the theorem is explicitly Everett-dependent.
Citation to preserve | [Wallace, arXiv:0906.2718](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2718); [Oxford Research Archive chapter record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:48119a06-9d03-4429-860e-fee1ff01047e).

Source | David Z. Albert, “Probability in the Everett Picture,” in *Many Worlds? Everett, Quantum Theory, and Reality* (Oxford University Press, 2010), pp. 355–368.
Problem it solves | Critically tests whether Everettian decision-theory arguments explain physical probability, typical observed frequencies, and empirical confirmation rather than merely constrain preferences under stipulated rationality axioms.
Premises | The Everett picture and the proposed Deutsch–Wallace identification of squared-amplitude branch weight with rational betting weight; comparison with alternative caring/decision measures.
Proof skeleton | Separate facts about what branches exist from normative facts about how an agent should care across them; construct or discuss alternative preference rules and low-weight observation sequences; argue that a rule of rational action does not automatically explain why observed frequencies confirm the theory or why low-weight branches should be regarded as unlikely.
What maps to this framework | The criticism transfers strongly: deriving an additive scalar readout or a rational valuation of records would still not derive how often records form, which possibility is selected, or why observed frequencies confirm the rule.
What does not map | The framework is not committed to Everettian branching, so Everett-specific identity/caring objections do not directly apply. Its admitted missing RATE is clearer than a failed attempt to disguise it as a utility.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Prove a separation theorem with three independently variable structures on the same trajectory set: record readout `S`, formation frequency/measure `μ`, and agent utility `u`. Exhibit models with the same `S` but different `μ`, and the same `μ` but different `u`.
Import risk | Medium as a conceptual objection, low as a logical separation template. Do not present one side of the continuing Everett debate as a settled no-go theorem.
Citation to preserve | [Albert chapter bibliographic record, doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199560561.003.0013](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199560561.003.0013); volume: [*Many Worlds?*](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199560561.001.0001).

Source | Wojciech H. Zurek, “Environment-Assisted Invariance, Entanglement, and Probabilities in Quantum Physics,” *Physical Review Letters* 90, 120404 (2003).
Problem it solves | Attempts to derive equal probabilities for envariantly swappable outcomes and then the squared-amplitude rule from symmetries of entangled pure states.
Premises | A system entangled with an environment; Schmidt decomposition and complex amplitudes; local unitary transformations on the system that can be undone by environment-only transformations; locality/no-signalling-style claims about unchanged local facts; probability assignments, certainty for Schmidt-supported alternatives, additivity/fine-graining, and continuity for general coefficients.
Proof skeleton | For equal Schmidt magnitudes, swap two system states and undo the swap on the environment; because the system’s local state is envariant, exchange-related outcomes receive equal probabilities. Fine-grain unequal coefficients into equal-amplitude subbranches, count them, and extend to irrational weights by continuity to obtain `p_k = |c_k|^2`.
What maps to this framework | Symmetry-based indifference is relevant to the proven free `C_11` action: symmetry can establish equality of any admissible weights across an orbit, while failing to choose a unique member. This cleanly distinguishes “equal treatment” from “actual selection.”
What does not map | There is no entangled global state, Schmidt coefficients, environment subsystem, reversible local unitaries, probability map, or fine-grained branch space. Cubic/translation covariance alone provides no numerical amplitude whose square could be a weight.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Prove an orbit-uniformity lemma: any normalized formation distribution invariant under a free transitive `C_11` action is uniform. Then state explicitly that existence and normalization of that distribution are imported; covariance alone neither asserts stochasticity nor selects a realized initial condition.
Import risk | High. The envariance literature disputes whether probability, outcome identity, additivity, or fine-graining assumptions do essential work. Used safely, it supplies a conditional symmetry theorem, not probability ex nihilo.
Citation to preserve | [Zurek, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 90, 120404 (2003), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.120404](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.120404).

Source | Maximilian Schlosshauer and Arthur Fine, “On Zurek’s Derivation of the Born Rule,” *Foundations of Physics* 35, 197–213 (2005).
Problem it solves | Audits the envariance derivation for implicit assumptions and identifies the gap between state symmetry and a probability conclusion.
Premises | Zurek’s entangled-state/envariance setup plus careful separation of physical-state invariance, ignorance, outcome probabilities, additivity, and measurement assumptions.
Proof skeleton | Reconstruct the equal-amplitude and fine-graining steps; ask which transformations leave which physical/probabilistic quantities invariant; show that envariance alone does not automatically establish the needed probability claims and that auxiliary assumptions carry nontrivial weight.
What maps to this framework | Provides the right audit discipline: every step from covariance of records or initial-condition orbits to equal weights, unique choices, or frequencies must name the extra bridge premise. It directly supports preserving the framework’s admission that weight and RATE are downstream.
What does not map | It is a critique of a quantum entanglement argument, not a theorem about cellular automata or permanent records. It does not rule out adding a well-motivated probability law later.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Dependency-check the finite proof in a proof assistant or explicit lemma DAG: group action → orbit equivalence; invariant normalized measure → equal weights; sampling law → frequencies; none of the arrows may be collapsed. Generate countermodels whenever a bridge premise is removed.
Import risk | Low as a proof-audit template; medium if described as conclusively refuting envariance rather than exposing assumptions in a debated derivation.
Citation to preserve | [Schlosshauer and Fine, *Found. Phys.* 35, 197–213 (2005), doi:10.1007/s10701-004-1941-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-004-1941-6); [arXiv:quant-ph/0312058](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312058).

## Recurrence, reversibility, and the transient/cycle split

Source | Poincaré recurrence theorem; modern statement in Peter Walters, *An Introduction to Ergodic Theory* (Springer, 1982), §1.4.
Problem it solves | Establishes recurrence for measure-preserving dynamics on a finite-measure space: almost every point in a positive-measure set returns to that set, in fact recurrently under standard formulations.
Premises | A finite measure space `(X, Σ, μ)`; a measurable transformation preserving `μ` (usually expressed as `μ(T⁻¹A) = μ(A)`); measurable sets. Exact return of individual states is not assumed in a continuum.
Proof skeleton | If a positive-measure subset of `A` never returned, its successive images/preimages would yield infinitely many pairwise disjoint sets of the same positive measure, contradicting finiteness of total measure. Apply the argument to nonreturning subsets to obtain almost-everywhere recurrence.
What maps to this framework | For a finite discrete runner with counting measure and bijective update, the theorem reduces to exact periodic return. It clarifies the extra premise hidden in “recurrence”: preservation/bijectivity, not merely determinism and finiteness.
What does not map | Poincaré recurrence is almost-everywhere return to neighborhoods/sets, not a universal first-return event, record-formation rule, or thermodynamic arrow. On the infinite `Z^3` lattice the total counting measure is infinite, so the standard theorem does not apply directly. A write-once record included in the full state prevents return to a pre-record state.
Runner/proof translation candidate | State and prove the finite lemma: if `X` is finite and `f: X → X` is bijective, every orbit is exactly periodic with period at most `|X|`. Then explicitly define whether the monitored/record register is part of `X`; if so, test bijectivity and recurrence again on the enlarged state space.
Import risk | High if recurrence is invoked from “finite deterministic” alone as if the rule were reversible, or transferred from the 11-site runner to the infinite lattice. Low as a conditional pigeonhole theorem.
Citation to preserve | [Walters, *An Introduction to Ergodic Theory*, doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-5775-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-5775-2).

Source | Olivier Martin, Andrew M. Odlyzko, and Stephen Wolfram, “Algebraic Properties of Cellular Automata,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 93, 219–258 (1984).
Problem it solves | Derives the state-transition structure of finite linear cellular automata, including transient trees feeding periodic attractor cycles.
Premises | A finite periodic lattice, finite alphabet/field, and specified linear local cellular-automaton rule. The general transient/cycle fact needs only a deterministic map on a finite set; the paper’s detailed counts use polynomial/algebraic structure.
Proof skeleton | Represent the global update by algebra over finite fields/polynomial quotients; decompose the state space according to factors of the update; derive cycle lengths, transient depths, and complete transition diagrams. At the elementary level, pigeonhole repetition gives eventual periodicity, and equality `x_i = x_j` makes all later states repeat with period `j − i`.
What maps to this framework | This directly models the 11-site “settling era then repeating era.” It supplies language and algebraic templates for proving the closed-form census and exact transient/period distributions for a specified rule.
What does not map | Eventual periodicity does not require or produce Poincaré measure preservation. If the global update is bijective, every component is a cycle and there are no transient trees; hence a genuine settling era diagnoses non-injectivity of the represented state update or omission/coarse-graining of state variables. The paper’s linear methods do not apply to an arbitrary nonlinear rule.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Build the functional graph of the supplied update; prove every component has one directed cycle with in-trees; derive symbolic formulas for preperiod μ and period λ and verify them exhaustively. For E2, formalize “first orbit admissibility” as a predicate on the cycle and determine whether it is computable from bounded local history.
Import risk | Low for the finite functional-graph lemma; medium-high for importing the paper’s linear algebra without proving the toy update is linear. Calling the transient “thermodynamic settling” would be an unsupported physical interpretation.
Citation to preserve | [Martin, Odlyzko, and Wolfram, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 93, 219–258 (1984), doi:10.1007/BF01223745](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01223745).

## Lawlike initial-condition proposals

Source | David Z. Albert, *Time and Chance* (Harvard University Press, 2000), especially the Past Hypothesis program.
Problem it solves | Explains macroscopic temporal asymmetries using time-reversal-invariant microscopic laws by postulating that the universe began in a very low-entropy macrostate and adding a typicality/statistical postulate over compatible microstates.
Premises | A microstate phase space and time-symmetric dynamics; a macrostate coarse graining; Boltzmann entropy from macroregion volume; the Past Hypothesis low-entropy initial macroregion; a Statistical Postulate/typicality measure conditional on that region.
Proof skeleton | Condition the initial microstate on a very small low-entropy macroregion; use the phase-space measure to show that typical compatible microstates evolve toward larger macroregions in the future; use the special boundary condition to block the time-reversed inference that entropy was higher in the past.
What maps to this framework | Demonstrates a respected strategy in which an initial condition is promoted to lawlike status without selecting a unique microstate. It suggests that a symmetry-invariant admissible initial *class* plus a separate typicality rule may be more plausible than a unique covariantly selected site/configuration.
What does not map | The framework supplies no macrovariables, entropy, phase-space volume, typicality measure, or low-entropy boundary condition. The Past Hypothesis does not derive its special macroregion from spatial symmetry, nor does it select a member of a free `C_11` orbit. It is an additional postulate plus a statistical measure, not a theorem of reversible dynamics.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Define an invariant “simple initial macroclass” `P ⊆ X`; prove `P` is a union of `C_11`-orbits and no equivariant map picks one point from a free orbit. Add and label a conditional measure on `P`, then calculate typical record histories. This separates lawlike restriction from microstate selection.
Import risk | Medium as an architectural analogy; very high if “low complexity” or “clean” is treated as entropy without a coarse graining and measure. The status of the Past Hypothesis as law rather than boundary fact remains philosophical and cosmological, not settled by the toy model.
Citation to preserve | [Albert, *Time and Chance*, Harvard University Press (2000), ISBN 9780674003173](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674003173/time-and-chance).

Source | James B. Hartle and Stephen W. Hawking, “Wave Function of the Universe,” *Physical Review D* 28, 2960–2975 (1983).
Problem it solves | Proposes a quantum state/boundary condition for a spatially closed universe via a path integral over compact regular geometries with only the observed three-geometry as boundary—the no-boundary proposal.
Premises | Quantum cosmology and the Wheeler–DeWitt framework; a gravitational and matter action; a functional integral over compact positive-definite four-geometries and regular matter configurations; choices of integration measure/contour and boundary data; semiclassical approximation in applications.
Proof skeleton | Define the wave functional by the Euclidean sum over compact interpolating geometries; show formally that it satisfies the Wheeler–DeWitt equation/boundary regularity; evaluate saddle points in a minisuperspace model; recover de Sitter-like classical behavior and amplitudes for alternative cosmological histories.
What maps to this framework | It exemplifies a serious attempt to make initial-state content lawlike rather than arbitrarily selecting a unique classical initial configuration. A discrete analogue could weight entire boundaryless histories and derive a distribution on boundary configurations.
What does not map | “No boundary” is not “no extra premise”: the action, path-integral contour/measure, field content, topology class, and quantum probability interpretation are substantial inputs. The lattice framework has fixed external time only downstream, no spacetime path integral, gravity, amplitudes, or Wheeler–DeWitt constraint. It cannot inherit no-boundary probabilities from site covariance.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Define a finite set of periodic/boundaryless spacetime histories compatible with the supplied rule and an explicitly stated invariant weight `w(h)`; push `w` forward to initial slices and test normalization and `C_11`-invariance. Prove that equal orbit weights still do not select a unique realized history.
Import risk | Very high as a direct import; medium as a model for “law plus boundary-state functional.” The proposal remains a quantum-cosmology research program, not an empirically established initial-condition law.

## Thermodynamic irreversibility of records

Source | Rolf Landauer, “Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process,” *IBM Journal of Research and Development* 5, 183–191 (1961).
Problem it solves | Connects logical irreversibility—many distinguishable logical inputs mapped to one output, especially reset/erasure—to unavoidable entropy export and, for an isothermal one-bit reset, a minimum heat scale `k_B T ln 2`.
Premises | Information is physically instantiated; a memory coupled to thermodynamic degrees of freedom/heat bath; logically irreversible compression of phase-space alternatives; cyclic or reset operation under suitable equilibrium/quasistatic assumptions for the familiar bound.
Proof skeleton | Model a bit by two distinguishable physical macrostates; resetting either value to one standard state halves the accessible logical phase volume and lowers memory entropy by `k_B ln 2`; the second law requires compensating entropy in the environment, giving at least `k_B T ln 2` heat in the ideal isothermal limit.
What maps to this framework | A lock that overwrites multiple possible local states with one fixed recorded state is logically irreversible if the pre-lock possibility cannot be recovered. Landauer demands an explicit physical sink/resource for that lost information. Write-once operation avoids repeated reset locally but consumes a supply of blank metastable sites.
What does not map | Landauer does not say measurement or writing necessarily costs `k_B T ln 2`; the canonical cost is for logically irreversible reset under thermodynamic assumptions. The record axiom contains no temperature, energy, bath, entropy, metastability, or physical implementation. “Permanent” is not derived from the erasure bound.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Extend the state with a record register and classify the lock map’s preimage multiplicities. Compute lost logical information `log₂|f⁻¹(y)|` per transition; under an explicitly added thermal implementation translate it to an entropy/heat lower bound. Track blank-record capacity and saturation time.
Import risk | Very high if the bound is asserted from abstract additivity or logical permanence alone. Low as a conditional implementation constraint once physical memory and a bath are specified.
Citation to preserve | [Landauer, *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 5, 183–191 (1961), doi:10.1147/rd.53.0183](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183).

Source | Charles H. Bennett, “Logical Reversibility of Computation,” *IBM Journal of Research and Development* 17, 525–532 (1973).
Problem it solves | Shows that irreversible computations can be simulated reversibly by retaining enough history and then “uncomputing” intermediate garbage, avoiding logically irreversible erasure during the computation.
Premises | Deterministic computation/Turing-machine model; reversible elementary transitions; auxiliary storage for input and history; ability to copy the final output in an appropriate classical basis and run the computation backward.
Proof skeleton | Simulate the forward computation while recording each step so the transition is injective; copy the desired output; reverse the saved computation to restore work tapes and remove garbage while retaining input and output. Time and storage overhead replace compulsory per-gate erasure.
What maps to this framework | A permanent record resembles Bennett’s retained history tape: it can make an otherwise many-to-one effective evolution reversible when the record is included in the full state. This gives a precise test of whether record creation stores the information apparently lost by the visible update.
What does not map | One record per site with permanent locks supplies finite local capacity, whereas indefinite reversible history retention generally needs growing memory or periodic uncomputation/erasure. Quantum no-cloning limits indiscriminate copying of unknown qubit states; only appropriately classical/orthogonal record variables can be copied as in the classical construction.
Runner/proof translation candidate | Define the augmented update `f_aug(x,r) = (f(x), r || g(x))`; test injectivity and find the minimal `g` that makes it reversible. Compare E1 and E2 by the number of history bits retained and prove when finite-site saturation destroys further write-once operation.
Import risk | Medium. Bennett is a constructive computability theorem, not a claim that nature actually stores all history or that records select outcomes. High if classical copying is applied to arbitrary unknown quantum possibilities.
Citation to preserve | [Bennett, *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 17, 525–532 (1973), doi:10.1147/rd.176.0525](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.176.0525).
