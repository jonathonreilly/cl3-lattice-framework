# Axiom-conversation dossier

## 1. The question and the method

### The question

Are the four axioms complete?

The evidence does **not** support an unqualified yes - and it now bounds
the question tightly from both sides. It does support a narrower result: W3 is derived and composed
at its stated fixture scope, conditional on 23 named supplies. That is a
substantial bounded closure, but it is not a derivation of those supplies from
the four axioms and it is not a beyond-fixture or global closure. The other
walls cannot be totaled honestly from the readable receipts in this extraction.

Accordingly, this dossier organizes evidence wall by wall; it does not turn
bounded theorems, residual counts, or sharply stated gaps into a completeness
verdict.

### Method

The campaign method is a sequence of stretch attempts coupled to a forcing
ledger.

- A **stretch attempt** tries to construct the wall's required content from the
  landed surface at an explicitly bounded scope. A failed attempt is useful
  only when it identifies what the surface lacks without silently importing
  that content.
- The **forcing ledger** records every required component as derived or
  supplied. It is updated when later work derives a formerly supplied
  component. W3 provides the readable example: LAW was derived in Cycle 743;
  ADMISS was derived conditionally in Cycle 747; ACTUAL was derived in Cycle
  750; BINDER was derived in Cycle 751; and the four predicates were composed
  in one acceptance call in Cycle 754.

This dossier uses four evidence classes:

- **DERIVED** — the required structure is constructed at the stated scope.
  Conditions and scope limits remain part of the claim.
- **COUNTED RESIDUAL** — forcing reduces the space to an explicitly counted
  non-unique remainder; the count is evidence, not a uniqueness claim.
- **ONE-SENTENCE GAP** — the missing content has been isolated precisely enough
  to state verbatim in one sentence.
- **REFINED WALL** — the original wall has been decomposed or narrowed, but not
  closed.

## 3. The pattern

The campaign-level pattern specified for this synthesis is:

- At the smallest tested scopes, forcing reaches complete structures: a unique
  genesis word length, a unique `b=1` program, and a unique occurrence
  survivor.
- Above those smallest scopes, the honest outcome is a counted residual rather
  than an asserted uniqueness result.
- The genuine missing content is not diffuse. Where forcing stops, the gap can
  be stated narrowly and, in the strongest cases, in one sentence.

The exact wall and cycle provenance for the three uniqueness results and the
above-scope residual counts are carried in the supervisor-filled rows and can be
used as a wall-by-wall completeness argument. They are retained here as the
structural pattern requested for the consolidated deliverable, not presented
as newly verified facts from the three W3 receipts.

W3 shows why the distinction matters. At Cycle 747, the absence of an
actual-member selector and the need for a formation surface were real,
separately recorded gaps. Later cycles supplied derivations for ACTUAL and
BINDER and then composed all four flags. Yet the capstone still carries 23
named conditions and an explicit fixture boundary. Thus “derived structure”
and “axioms complete without qualifications” are different claims.

On the evidence presently readable, the most defensible structural conclusion
is therefore conditional: the axioms and landed semantics exert enough pressure
to force complete structures at certain minimal scopes and to sharply reduce
larger spaces, but residuals, stated gaps, conditions, and scope boundaries must
remain visible. The small number and precision of genuine gaps makes the
program testable; it does not by itself settle completeness.
