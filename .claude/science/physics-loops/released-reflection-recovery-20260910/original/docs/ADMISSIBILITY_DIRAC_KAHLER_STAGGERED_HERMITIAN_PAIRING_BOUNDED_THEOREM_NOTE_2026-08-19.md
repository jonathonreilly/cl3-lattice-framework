## 3. The Split And The Staggered Involution

This section is the headline. It concerns the pair \((H_q,K_{ij})\), and its
conclusion is a closed form.

**The split.** Block 142's atlas-global identity
\(Q_{ij}+Q_{ij}^{\dagger}=2mH_q\) says exactly that the Hermitian half of

\[
 \boxed{K_{ij}\ \text{is REAL ANTISYMMETRIC and $m$-FREE on all 16 edges.}}
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

Both properties are load-bearing. Realness is what makes the current

\[
 \boxed{J_{ij}:=H_q^{-1}K_{ij}}                                \tag{8}

an \(H_q\)-skew operator over the rationals, and \(m\)-freeness is what makes

**THE SPECTRAL FACT.** On all 16 ordered edges,

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{charpoly}(J_{ij})\ \text{is EVEN, INVERTIBLE, and
        SQUAREFREE},\qquad 16/16,}                             \tag{9}
\]

and this is **forced structurally**, not observed edgewise: \(H_q\) is
positive definite and \(K_{ij}\) is real antisymmetric, so \(J_{ij}\) is

**THE STAGGERED SITE PARITY.** Define \(X_0\) by (3): the diagonal operator

\[

## 4. Uniqueness And The Hermiticity Criterion

**THE CRITERION.** Let \(\Theta\) be \(H_q\)-self-adjoint. Then, for symbolic
\(m\),

\[
 \boxed{\Theta^{\dagger}Q_{ij}\ \text{is Hermitian}
        \iff \{\Theta,J_{ij}\}=0.}                             \tag{13}
\]

The forward direction is the computation of Section 3 read backwards; the
converse is forced because the mass term and the current term must be
Hermitian separately, \(m\) being symbolic. Equation (13) reduces the search
for a Hermitian curved pairing to a linear anticommutation problem, and it is
the reason the scout of Section 5 fails while \(X_0\) succeeds.

**THE UNIQUENESS THEOREM.** Impose (13) simultaneously on all sixteen
currents. The solution space is as small as it can be:

\[
 \boxed{\dim\bigl\{\,Y:\{Y,J_{ij}\}=0\ \text{for all 16 edges}\,\bigr\}=1,}
                                                               \tag{14}
\]

so

\[
 \boxed{X_0\ \text{is UNIQUE up to sign.}}                      \tag{15}
\]

The one-dimensionality is exact, computed on the common anticommutant of the
full sixteen-edge family, not on a single edge. On a single edge the
anticommutant is 16-dimensional — that is Section 7's \(X_0\mathbb Q[J]\) —
and the atlas-global requirement is what cuts it to a line.

Equations (14)--(15) are stronger than an existence statement. They say the
displayed atlas admits exactly one metric-adapted Hermitian pairing up to
sign, so there is no family of Hermitian involutions to search over for
positivity, and no freedom left to spend. Whatever positivity fails to hold
for \(X_0\) at operator level cannot be repaired by choosing a different
atlas-global anticommuting involution, because there is none.

The criterion (13) also fixes the perimeter. It quantifies over
\(H_q\)-self-adjoint operators paired with the displayed currents. It says
nothing about pairings built from a different metric, from a completed
action, or from a different carrier.
