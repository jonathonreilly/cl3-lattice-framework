## 4. No Finite-Range Logarithm Of The Exact Shift

Consider first one scalar component on `Z`. A translation-invariant
finite-range generator has

\[
 D=\sum_{r=-R}^{R}d_rT^r,
 \qquad d(k)=\sum_{r=-R}^{R}d_re^{ikr}.
\]

Its symbol is continuous and `2 pi` periodic. If its unit flow were the exact
one-site shift, then

\[
 e^{d(k)}=e^{ik}.
\]

Consequently `d(k)-ik` lies in `2 pi i Z`. Continuity makes that integer
constant. But periodicity gives `d(k+2 pi)=d(k)`, whereas `i(k+2 pi)` differs
from `ik` by `2 pi i`. Contradiction.

Equivalently, `exp(d(k))` has a globally defined periodic logarithm and hence
winding zero, while `exp(ik)` has winding one. A periodic logarithm has
winding zero. On six components the determinant of an exact common shift has
winding six, so finite component mixing does not repair the determinant
obstruction.

The centered difference `D=(T-T^-1)/2` is a valid finite-range
anti-Hermitian generalized generator and commutes with many lattice actions.
Its exponential has winding zero and is not the one-site shift. Therefore the
theorem does not forbid a generalized lattice symmetry; it forbids calling a
bounded-local autonomous generator the exact continuous logarithm of the
discrete shift.

## 5. No Compact Same-Carrier Exact Chain Pullback

Let the existing carrier have finite component dimension `m`, and let a
same-carrier pullback from lattice coefficients be

\[
 I_Wf(X)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}W(X-j)f_j,
\]

where `W` is a differentiable, compactly supported, nonzero `m x m` matrix
kernel. Suppose a finite-range matrix-convolution generator `D` on the same
coefficient carrier obeys the exact chain identity

\[
 \partial_X I_Wf=-I_W(Df)
\]

for every finitely supported vector-valued `f`. With
`D(xi)=sum_r D_r exp(i r xi)`, Fourier transformation gives

\[
 \widehat W(\xi)\left(D(\xi)+i\xi I_m\right)=0.
\]

Compact support makes every entry of the Fourier transform entire. Because
`W` is nonzero and Fourier transformation is injective, its Fourier transform
is entire and not identically zero. At least one entry is therefore nonzero
on some real interval `U`. On `U`, the matrix `W_hat(xi)` has a nonzero row,
so the chain identity forces

\[
 \det\left(D(\xi)+i\xi I_m\right)=0.
\]

The determinant is entire, hence the identity theorem extends this equality
to every `xi`. Fix any real `xi_0`. Periodicity of the finite Laurent matrix
symbol gives `D(xi_0+2 pi n)=D(xi_0)`, while

\[
 p(n)=\det\left(D(\xi_0)+i(\xi_0+2\pi n)I_m\right)
\]

is a degree-`m` polynomial in `n` with nonzero leading coefficient
`(i 2 pi)^m`. The extended equality would make `p(n)=0` for every integer
`n`, an impossibility. This proof requires neither a nonzero-integral premise,
a chosen finite-difference stencil, nor a finite candidate census.

The proof permits arbitrary finite component mixing in both `W` and `D`.
Applying it at `m=6` covers the complete actual raw staggered tensor carrier,
