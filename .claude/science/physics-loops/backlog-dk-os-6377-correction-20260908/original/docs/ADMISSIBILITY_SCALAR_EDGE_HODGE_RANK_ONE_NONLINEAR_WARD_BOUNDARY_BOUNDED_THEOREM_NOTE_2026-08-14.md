## 7. Separable Rank-One Class Boundary

The two rows reveal a class invariant. Let the free scalar symbol use the
same even finite-Laurent function `f` on the two tested spatial axes. Require

\[
 f(k_\mu+q_\mu)-f(k_\mu)

\]

not merely the summed identity (10), and require the action-compatible
zero-transfer generator `d(s)=u(s,0)`. Since `p(q)=q+O(q^3)`, the
zero-transfer limit of (26a) gives `d(s)=f'(s)/2`. Take the same quarter
transfer in both axes and parameterize

\[
 k(s)=(s,-s-\pi/2,0,0).                          \tag{26b}
\]

Evenness and the common `f` make every `s` an equal-level row. Equation (10)
then forces

\[
 u_y=-u_x.                                        \tag{27}
\]

The pure stress is consequently always proportional to

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.         \tag{28}
\]

Its magnitude may vary with matter momentum, but its geometry contraction
has only one shared response coefficient. Wherever the stress is nonzero,
the analogue of (22) requires

\[
 d(s+\pi/2)-d(s)=C                                \tag{29}
\]

for one constant `C` wherever the stress is nonzero. Every finite Laurent
symbol is real analytic. The quarter-transfer stress cannot vanish
identically: if it did, (26a) would make `f(s+pi/2)=f(s)`; differentiating and
taking the zero-transfer limit would make the action-compatible `d` also
quarter-periodic, contrary to the nonzero increment below. Its zeros are
therefore isolated. Continuity extends (29) across them. Applying (29) four
times and using periodicity gives

\[
 4C=d(s+2\pi)-d(s)=0.                             \tag{30}
\]

Thus `C=0`. But the executed nonzero quarter-shift generator increment is

\[

\]

For (13), the difference is exactly one. Equations (30) and (31) contradict.

This theorem is restricted to the isotropic, separable, even finite-Laurent
pure-rank-one divided-difference class with action-compatible `d` and a
