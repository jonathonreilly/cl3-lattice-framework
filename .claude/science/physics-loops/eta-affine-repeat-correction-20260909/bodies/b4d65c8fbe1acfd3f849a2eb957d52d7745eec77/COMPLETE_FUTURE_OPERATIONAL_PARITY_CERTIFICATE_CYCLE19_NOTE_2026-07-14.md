## 1. The Operational Object Being Defined

Fix one exact law `L`, one cut in a history `h`, and a declared family `T` of
finite compatible future protocols. A protocol includes its interventions,
causal order, readable output records, and conditioning convention. Define

```text
F_(L,T)(h; J) = conditional law of every future record transcript
                when J in T is applied after h.

h ~_(L,T) h'
iff F_(L,T)(h; J) = F_(L,T)(h'; J) for every J in T.
```

This is an equivalence relation. Its class `[h]_(L,T)` is a predictive state:
it retains exactly the distinctions the declared futures can expose.

Two facts are immediate and load-bearing.

First, the quotient is **relative** to `L` and `T`. If `T_1` is contained in
`T_2`, then equivalence under `T_2` implies equivalence under `T_1`; adding a
tester may split a class but cannot merge classes that were already separated.

Second, this quotient does not by itself define the axiom's locked local
possibility. Qubit says the local possibilities are distinguished by the
supplied algebraic structure, and Record says one admissible local possibility
is locked. Two algebraically distinct projectors can be predictively
equivalent under a restricted future repertoire. That makes them one
predictive class relative to that repertoire; it does not erase their
algebraic distinction unless the framework deliberately changes what
"content" means.

The safe use is therefore:

```text
complete-future equivalence -> definition of predictive state or protocol role

not automatically

complete-future equivalence -> identity of every locked local possibility.
```

## 2. A Role Definition That Does Not Name `X`

For a signed center record `r` and two future endpoint records `l,c`, define

```text
ParityCertificate(r ; l,c)
iff for every lawful branch of the declared endpoint-Z protocol,
    sign(r) = sign(l) sign(c).
```

Equivalently, in bit labels,

```text
s_center = (-1)^(z_left xor z_right).
```

This is an operational definition because its truth is fixed by readable
record transcripts. It is role-specific: it says what this record certifies
in this protocol. It does not say every record is a parity certificate, that
parity is generic ontology, or that the center record exhausts the entire
future state.

The definition also separates two questions:

- **extension:** which actual candidate records satisfy it; and
- **intension:** what the words "parity certificate" mean.

Fixing the intension by definition does not prove extension. A center stage
marker can exist without certifying parity. The exact law must generate the
relation or include it as an explicit, falsifiable field.

## 3. Exact Name-Free Decoder Theorem

Prepare the three-site linear cluster

```text
|C3> = CZ_ab CZ_bc |+ + +>.
```

It obeys

```text
Z_a X_b Z_c |C3> = |C3>.
```

For an arbitrary Hermitian qubit involution at the center,

```text
A = n_x X + n_y Y + n_z Z,
n_x^2+n_y^2+n_z^2=1,
```

direct evaluation gives

```text
<C3| Z_a A_b Z_c |C3> = n_x.
```

The signed center result and endpoint-`Z` product are binary variables. If one
is a deterministic certificate of the other, the correlation has magnitude
one. Therefore `|n_x|=1`, and unit norm forces

```text
n_y=n_z=0,
A=+X or -X.
```

The signs exchange outcome names and leave the unordered PVM unchanged.

The companion runner checks the stronger transcript statement. Center `X`
measurement produces

```text
center + : |Phi+> = (|00>+|11>)/sqrt(2)
center - : |Psi+> = (|01>+|10>)/sqrt(2),
```

each with probability `1/2`. The later endpoint-`Z` records give four joint
transcripts, each with probability `1/4`, and the center sign fixes endpoint
parity. A center `Y` measurement gives eight joint transcripts, each with
probability `1/8`; both endpoint parities occur for either center sign.

Thus the operational predicate selects the center PVM without putting `X`
into that predicate. This is exactly the good part of the Cycle-18 route.

What it does not do is prepare `|C3>`, supply the center event, supply the
endpoint decoder, normalize branch weights, or prove that the candidate law
has defined its center stage by this predicate.

## 4. The Decisive Paired Protocol

The risk in saying "content is what every future read reveals" is quietly
letting the one desired future count as every future. The minimum exact
control uses the same center sign and endpoint parity table but changes the
phase information available before endpoint records form.

### Protocol Q: coherent parity certificate

Conditioned on the permanent center sign, leave the endpoints in

```text
rho_Q(+) = |Phi+><Phi+|,
rho_Q(-) = |Psi+><Psi+|.
```

### Protocol C: dephased parity certificate

Conditioned on the same permanent center sign, dephase in the endpoint `Z`
basis:

```text
rho_C(+) = (|00><00| + |11><11|)/2,
rho_C(-) = (|01><01| + |10><10|)/2.
```

### Restricted endpoint-`Z` future

For either protocol,

```text
p(+;00)=p(+;11)=1/4,
p(-;01)=p(-;10)=1/4,
```

and every other signed endpoint-`Z` transcript has probability zero. Both
protocols pass `PC` exactly. If this is the only admitted future test, they are
operationally equivalent relative to that restricted repertoire.

### One separating future

Before endpoint `Z` records form, read endpoint `XX` parity. Equivalently use
the effect

```text
E_XX+ = (I + X tensor X)/2.
```

Then

```text
Tr[rho_Q(s) E_XX+] = 1,
Tr[rho_C(s) E_XX+] = 1/2
```

for either center sign `s`. A sign-conditioned Bell effect gives the same
`1` versus `1/2` separation. The sixteen two-qubit Pauli products span the
full matrix space, so a finite tomographically complete repertoire certainly
separates the two conditional instruments.

This pair proves only the needed narrow point:

> Equality of the complete center/endpoint-`Z` transcript and satisfaction of
> `PC` do not imply equality under a larger physically legal future
> intervention repertoire.

It does not prove the `XX` tester is legal in the final bare-metal law. That is
precisely why the legal repertoire must be part of the exact operational
specification. If the law permits only endpoint-`Z` continuations at this cut,
the pair is deliberately equivalent there. If the law permits a phase-
sensitive record before the endpoint `Z` commit, the pair is distinct.

### The cut matters

After the endpoint `Z` records actually form, their projective read dephases
the coherent branch into the classical parity branch. From that later cut the
two displayed endpoint carriers agree. Complete-future equivalence must be
anchored to a stated history cut; it cannot silently move from "after center
record" to "after endpoint records."

## 5. Why Scalar Readout Does Not Supply The Quotient

The Record axiom gives a direction of dependence:

```text
record content -> readout value I.
```

It does not give the converse. No injectivity clause says that equal scalar
values imply equal contents.

For a finite disjoint record configuration `R`, both rules

```text
I_count(R) = number of records in R

I_code(R)  = sum over records of a content-dependent code value
```

are content-only, additive, and obey `I(empty)=0`. `I_count` assigns the same
one-record scalar to `center+` and `center-`; `I_code` may distinguish them.
Both satisfy the displayed algebraic shape of Record. Therefore scalar
equality cannot be used as identity of content or as complete operational
equivalence without an additional injectivity/separation theorem.

This does not make scalar `I` defective. It means `I` is one readable
functional, not automatically a tomography of every record type or process.

## 6. Why Permanence Does Not Supply The Quotient

Permanence says an existing record does not change. Both Protocol Q and
Protocol C can keep the signed center record fixed while later appending
endpoint or phase-test records. The two protocols can therefore agree on
permanence and disagree on a future transcript.

In bare-metal language:

```text
permanent means "this written fact does not get overwritten"

not

permanent means "this fact is a causally complete description of everything
that can still be written."
```

The second claim is predictive sufficiency. Under the live Qualification, "a
state is a configuration of records," it must become a theorem of the exact
law: histories with the same complete record configuration must give the same
conditional law for every admitted finite future protocol. In finite-state
language, every record fibre must be strongly lumpable under every context and
output.

The coherent/dephased control makes the repair options explicit:

1. a persistent preparation/process record distinguishes the two histories
   and the exact law uses it in future phase-sensitive records;
2. the exact law proves the apparent hidden distinction is inaccessible under
   every legal future, so the histories genuinely quotient together; or
3. the state Qualification is widened.

The first two are live routes. The present probe does not recommend the third.
It does show that merely pronouncing the center record permanent does not
choose among them.
