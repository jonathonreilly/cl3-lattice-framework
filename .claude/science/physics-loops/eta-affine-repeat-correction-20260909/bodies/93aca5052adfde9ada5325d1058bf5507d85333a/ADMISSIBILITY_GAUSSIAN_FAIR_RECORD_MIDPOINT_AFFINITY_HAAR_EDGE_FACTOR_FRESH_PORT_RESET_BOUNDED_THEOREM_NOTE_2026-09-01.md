## 2. Gaussian content gives a fair Record bit

### Supplied law

Write the eight real coordinates of \(A-C\in M_2(\mathbb C)\) with density

\[
d\mu_C(A)=\pi^{-4}
  \exp\!\left(-\lVert A-C\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\right)d^8A,
  \qquad C=C^\dagger .
\]

Thus each real coordinate has density
\(\pi^{-1/2}e^{-x^2}\), mean zero, and variance \(1/2\). Formation of a
Record carrying this content is GAUSSIAN_FORMATION.

### Derived uniformizer and bit

Because a Hermitian \(C\) has real trace,

\[
S(A):=\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}A
\]

is the sum of the two independent imaginary diagonal coordinates of \(A-C\).
Consequently

\[
S(A)\sim N(0,1)
\]

for every Hermitian center \(C\). If \(\Phi\) is the standard normal
cumulative distribution function, the probability-integral transform gives

\[
U(A):=\Phi(S(A))\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1].
\]

Define the fixed, content-only readout

\[
B(A):=\mathbf 1\{U(A)\leq\tfrac12\}.
\]

The boundary has zero Gaussian measure, so

\[
\Pr_{\mu_C}(B=0)=\Pr_{\mu_C}(B=1)=\tfrac12
\]

for every Hermitian \(C\). The threshold is fixed and independent of the
center, the target program, the later outcome, and \(\lambda\). The derivation
uses the displayed Gaussian density and its cumulative distribution only.
No trace/Born probability-weight rule appears in this calculation; the trace
inside \(S(A)\) is only the displayed scalar content statistic.
It also respects unitary changes of content coordinates because
\(\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}(VAV^\dagger)
=\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}A\).

## 5. Midpoint affinity on the complete ball gives real separate affinity

Let \(D=\{r\in\mathbb R^3:\lVert r\rVert\leq1\}\), supplied as the complete
preparation and test domain. Let \(q:D\times D\to\mathbb R\) be bounded and
satisfy the two midpoint identities from Section 4.

Fix \(r_0,r_1,s\) and put

\[
g(t)=q((1-t)r_0+tr_1,s),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]

The midpoint identity implies the Jensen midpoint equation for \(g\).
Induction gives

\[
g(k/2^j)
=\left(1-\frac{k}{2^j}\right)g(0)
 +\frac{k}{2^j}g(1)
\]

for every dyadic \(k/2^j\) in \([0,1]\).

For completeness, boundedness supplies the continuity step. If a
midpoint-affine function \(f\) is bounded in magnitude by \(M\) on a segment,
then whenever \(x,x+2^nh\) stay in that segment,

\[
f(x+h)-f(x)
=2^{-n}\bigl(f(x+2^nh)-f(x)\bigr),
\]

and hence its magnitude is at most \(2M\,2^{-n}\). Fix an interior point and
a positive distance \(\delta\) to the segment boundary. For each sufficiently
small nonzero \(h\), choose \(n=n(h)\) so that
\(\delta/2<2^n|h|\leq\delta\). Then \(n(h)\to\infty\) as \(h\to0\), while the
amplified point stays in the segment. This proves interior continuity.
Density of the dyadics now yields

\[
g(t)=(1-t)g(0)+tg(1)
\]

for every real \(t\in[0,1]\). Repeating with the other slot proves real
separate affinity on all of \(D\times D\).

The complete domain is load-bearing here. Finite or merely dyadic program
reachability supplies the dyadic identities, while COMPLETE_OPERATIONAL_DOMAIN supplies the
nondyadic points and the bounded response on which the continuity argument
acts.

\[
q(r,s)=c+a\mathbin{\cdot}r+d\mathbin{\cdot}s+r^TMs.
\]

Indeed, affinity in \(r\) writes \(q(r,s)=\alpha(s)+A(s)\mathbin{\cdot}r\);

## 6. Proper-cubic covariance gives the dot-product form

CUBIC_COVARIANCE requires

\[
q(Rr,Rs)=q(r,s)
\]

for every proper rotation \(R\) of the cube. The linear coefficients \(a\)
and \(d\) would have to be vectors fixed by every such rotation. The
half-turns about the coordinate axes leave no nonzero common fixed vector, so
\(a=d=0\).

For the bilinear coefficient, covariance gives

\[
R^TMR=M.
\]

The coordinate-axis half-turns force every off-diagonal entry of \(M\) to
vanish. Quarter-turns about the coordinate axes equate its three diagonal
entries. Hence \(M=bI\), and

\[
\boxed{q(r,s)=c+b\,r\mathbin{\cdot}s}.
\]

On pure inputs \(n,m\in S^2\), the operational bound \(0\leq q\leq1\) for
every \(n\mathbin{\cdot}m\in[-1,1]\) gives

\[
0\leq c-|b|,
\qquad
c+|b|\leq1.
\]

\[
\lambda=\frac bc
\]

therefore gives \(\lambda\in[-1,1]\). The constant case \(b=0\) is
\(\lambda=0\). The candidate supplies a parameter value within this full

## 7. Fixed-reference Haar typing and normalized one-edge law

Let \(\mu\) be the supplied normalized Haar probability measure on \(S^2\):

\[
\mu(S^2)=1,\qquad
\int_{S^2}n\,\mu(dn)=0,\qquad
\int_{S^2}n_i n_j\,\mu(dn)=\frac{\delta_{ij}}3.
\]

For a fixed pure neighbor \(m\),

\[
\int_{S^2}q(n,m)\,\mu(dn)
=c+b\,m\mathbin{\cdot}\int n\,\mu(dn)
=c.
\]

The fixed-reference normalizer is thus independent of \(m\), and the typed
Radon--Nikodym density is

\[
\phi_\lambda(n,m)
=\frac{q(n,m)}c
=1+\lambda\,n\mathbin{\cdot}m.
\]

For every \(\lambda\in[-1,1]\), this density is nonnegative and satisfies

\[
\int\phi_\lambda(n,m)\,\mu(dn)=1.
\]

It defines the transition

\[
T_m(E)=\int_E\phi_\lambda(n,m)\,\mu(dn).
\]

Symmetry of the dot product gives detailed balance,

\[
\mu(dm)T_m(dn)=\mu(dn)T_n(dm),
\]

so \(\mu\) is stationary and the edge law is reversible. At
\(\lambda=1\) the density vanishes only at \(n=-m\); at \(\lambda=-1\) it
vanishes only at \(n=m\). These singleton zero sets have Haar measure zero.
At \(\lambda=0\), \(T_m=\mu\).

### Joined Record-and-edge fine cylinder

Fix the supplied branch programs \(s_0,s_1\in S^2\). The Record-first

\[

=T_{s_b}(dn)

\]

Keeping the Gaussian content, branch Record, and outcome direction in one

\[
\widehat\nu_C(dA,db,dn)
=\mu_C(dA)\,
  \mathbf 1_{\{b=B(A)\}}\,

\]

Marginalizing the Gaussian content while retaining the branch Record gives

\[
\boxed{\nu(db,dn)
=\frac12\phi_\lambda(n,s_b)\mu(dn)}.
\]

Its total mass is one because each branch set has Gaussian mass \(1/2\) and
each \(T_{s_b}\) is normalized. Marginalizing the branch Record gives

\[
\sum_{b=0}^1\nu(db,dn)
=\frac{\phi_\lambda(n,s_0)+\phi_\lambda(n,s_1)}2\,\mu(dn)
=\phi_\lambda\!\left(n,\frac{s_0+s_1}{2}\right)\mu(dn).
\]

The last equality uses the already-derived affine formula on the complete
Bloch ball. Section 9 reuses this same \(\nu\) at every translated port; that

### Fixed-reference gauge control

The fixed measure and normalizer carry real content. On the full ball define

\[
g(m)=1+\lVert m\rVert^2,\qquad
\widetilde\phi(n,m)=g(m)\phi_\lambda(n,m).
\]

Every pure \(m\) has \(g(m)=2\), so normalizing separately on the pure menu

\[

\]

\[

\qquad
\widetilde\phi(n,0)=1.
\]

Thus pure-menu conditional tables leave a multiplicative type ambiguity on the

## 8. Rooted-tree gluing and topology controls

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite tree, choose an arbitrary root \(o\), and orient
each edge away from it. Define

\[
\mathbb P_G(dn_V)
=\mu(dn_o)\prod_{u\to v}T_{n_u}(dn_v).
\]

Expanding the transitions gives

\[
\mathbb P_G(dn_V)
=\left(\prod_{v\in V}\mu(dn_v)\right)
 \left(\prod_{\{u,v\}\in E}\phi_\lambda(n_u,n_v)\right).
\]

This is normalized for every finite tree: integrate a leaf first, use
\(\int\phi_\lambda(n_{\mathrm{leaf}},m)\mu(dn_{\mathrm{leaf}})=1\), remove
that leaf, and iterate to the root. Detailed balance permits reversing any
oriented edge, so the expanded joint law is independent of the chosen root.

At a vertex with fixed neighbors \(m_1,\ldots,m_k\), its local conditional
