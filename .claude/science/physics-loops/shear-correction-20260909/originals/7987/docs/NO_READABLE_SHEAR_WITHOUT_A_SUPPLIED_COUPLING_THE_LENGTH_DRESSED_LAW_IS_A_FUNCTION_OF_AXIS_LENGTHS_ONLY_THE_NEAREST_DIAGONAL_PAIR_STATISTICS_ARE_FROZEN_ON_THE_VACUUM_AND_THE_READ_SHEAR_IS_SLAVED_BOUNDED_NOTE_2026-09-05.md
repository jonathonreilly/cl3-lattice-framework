**Reading, not theorem.** In plain words: the sea is a pattern of records, and everything anyone can count in it is how often a site carries a record and how often two, three or four sites carry records together. Stretch the cubic cells along their own axes and those counts change. Slide one face sideways over another -- a shear -- and nothing changes at all, because the only lengths a law that hops between touching sites can feel are the lengths of the cube's edges, and sliding a face sideways lengthens only its diagonals. So the question becomes whether some cleverer count, or some more disturbed pattern, could feel it anyway. It cannot, and the reason is upstream of every count: the shear is not in the arithmetic that produces the pattern, so no function of the pattern can depend on it. The counts on the diagonals have a second and quite separate peculiarity on the undisturbed sea: they are not just zero but stuck at zero, so that even a coupling built by hand to move them moves them by nothing. Other counts are not stuck -- corners read while conditioned on a third record are alive, and diagonals wake up near a wall, near a conditioned record, or after the tick has run -- but every one of them is deaf to a shear for the same upstream reason. Finally, if one simply declares that the metric *is* what the counts say, then the sheared part of it is not free: it follows from the stretched part by a fixed rule. Along the coordinate axes and the body diagonal that dependence is a change of coordinates and costs nothing; at generic directions it is not, and the declared reading then distorts what it reads. Record licenses the counting; it supplies no statistic, no rate, no dressing and no coupling, and nothing here is derived from an axiom.
