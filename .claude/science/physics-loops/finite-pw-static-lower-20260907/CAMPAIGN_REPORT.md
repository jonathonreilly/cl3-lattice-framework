## The finite-qubit bridge became more honest and more useful

The [finite-transporter theorem, PR8031](https://github.com/jonathonreilly/qubit-lattice-axiom-framework/pull/8031), shows that the complete-irrep cutoff retains exact continuous gauge covariance. It cannot retain an exactly unitary fundamental transporter matrix on a finite nonzero carrier. For the actual cutoff, the matrix-unitarity defect has norm one at every cutoff size; the worst-case witness lives on the highest retained representation shell.

That obstruction does not prevent energy-controlled accuracy. For a path using each link once, the squared output error is bounded by the total initial path kinetic energy divided by the shell energy. The shell energy grows quadratically with cutoff. This statement includes arbitrary entanglement and explicitly prices normalization. It is consistent with established finite quantum-link models, whose transporter operators need not satisfy the full unitary link algebra.

[Block42, PR8032](https://github.com/jonathonreilly/qubit-lattice-axiom-framework/pull/8032), addresses the harder energy question rather than assuming state-norm closeness is enough. The new finite-cutoff charged trial estimate uses a local commutator and an explicit derivative bound. Distant faces cancel exactly. For a fixed path, the upper bound approaches the full-unitary path trial cost 4d/a uniformly in the surrounding volume. A directly computed R=1 single-plaquette example shows why the extra work matters: the naive exact energy identity fails after compression, while the corrected estimate bounds the actual error. The optional crude sufficient cutoff bound fails on that fixed example; the sharper actual-energy bound succeeds, and that limitation is preserved.

[Final block43, PR8033](https://github.com/jonathonreilly/qubit-lattice-axiom-framework/pull/8033), redoes the confinement lower-bound argument inside each finite cutoff, subtracting that cutoff’s own vacuum energy. Its constants remain uniform in cutoff and volume. Combining it with42 gives a positive lower and finite upper energy-per-distance range when the cutoff grows sufficiently fast with path length. It does not prove that the charged spectral minimum converges or equals the full-unitary trial cost.

## What the continuum boundary tells us

Here the controlled regime has small u=av: the electric term dominates the magnetic plaquette perturbation. The proven correlation length stays bounded in lattice units. When lattice spacing shrinks, an exponential in lattice separation beats every polynomial local-field normalization. Consequently this class of fields has vanishing connected correlations at distinct physical points.

This is a useful boundary for the research program. A nonzero separated-point continuum field would have to escape at least one stated hypothesis—for example by reaching a regime with a diverging correlation length in lattice units, or by proving a different admissible field/support scaling. No success of either route is presumed. The explicit Haar example also prevents an overclaim: nonzero Gaussian contact finite-dimensional distributions exist even in the product ground state, while propagating field dynamics are not established.
